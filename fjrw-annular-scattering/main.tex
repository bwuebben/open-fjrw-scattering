\begin{abstract}
Gross--Siebert mirrors are built from integral-affine bases with
singularities; we ask whether open Landau--Ginzburg wall-crossing gives one.
For the open FJRW theory of $x^4+y^4$ of Gross, Kelly and Tessler (GKT) it
does, on an annular cover, but its wall structure is not GKT
wall-crossing.  Send $x^ay^b$ to $z^{F(a,b)}$, for $F$ linear
sending $(4,0)$, $(0,4)$ to the rays of $\{X=0\}$, $\{Y=0\}$ in the
fan lattice of $\PP(1,1,2)$, the exponent lattice of the
Carl--Pumperla--Siebert base.  The exponents of the two first-order
descendent GKT generators become fractional and span an overlattice of index
eight.  The minimal cover preserving it is an integral-affine annulus with
two focus--focus singularities, two $z\mapsto z^2$ pullbacks of one, and
hyperbolic core holonomy of trace $18$.  The slab-function roots that lift
the monodromy have an infinite transport orbit and rescale
$dx\wedge dy$, so its Poisson bracket glues only as a Jacobi bracket on a
line bundle.  The generators produce walls, consistent at both
joints to all orders, placed by a convention symmetric in $x$ and $y$ along
the images of the exponents under an auxiliary gradient map.

The GKT Lie algebra embeds in the Hamiltonian derivations of this bracket,
but on the first ray carrying both descendent parameters the wall
coefficient is $-3/4$ where GKT's is $1/4$.  The potentials $W^{\min}$,
$W^{\max}$ of GKT's two extremal boundary conditions have, for each set of
labels, only the balanced-graph coefficient of least, respectively greatest,
$x$-degree nonzero.  For two labels with one descendent each, the diagram
carries $W^{\min}$ to a potential differing from $W^{\max}$ by the flow of
one GKT generator.  The normal form keeping only the coefficient of greatest
$x$-degree removes the difference; its geometric realization is open.  GKT's
canonical multisection homotopies can be chosen compatibly as the set of
labels grows.
\end{abstract}
