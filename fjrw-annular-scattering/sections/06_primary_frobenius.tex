\section{The primary Frobenius comparison}
\label{sec:primary-frobenius}

The theta multiplication of Theorem~\ref{thm:theta-multiplication} is defined
before enumerative normalization.  The factorization of
Theorem~\ref{thm:all-order-decoration} identifies the enumerative subfactors
in a noncommutative wall group.  Neither construction alone determines a
Frobenius structure.  The comparison also includes the primitive form, oscillatory periods,
residue pairing, and Kodaira--Spencer basis.

Work first on the ordered-residue cover, retaining labels and the two
coordinates.  General annular transport carries the primitive form in
$\Lcal_\kappa$, while every enumerative or residual GKT factor is a
volume-preserving right equivalence.  Both act on Brieskorn complexes.  We
construct the product and period-normalized basis in this labelled setting,
restrict to the narrow primary object, and only then apply the symmetric
idempotent of \eqref{eq:pushforwards}.

Suppress the descendent index in the primary variables and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primary-base}
 A_{\mathrm{prim}}
 =\QQ[[t_{a,b}:0\leq a,b\leq2]],
 \qquad \mathfrak m_{\mathrm{prim}}=(t_{a,b}).
\end{equation}
Statements over this complete ring mean the compatible systems over finite
quotients closed under the dependencies of Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}.

\subsection{The classical vertex and coefficient layers}

At the Fermat point the primary state space is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fermat-Jacobian}
 H_{4,4}=\QQ[X,Y]/(X^3,Y^3),
 \qquad e_{a,b}=[X^aY^b],\quad 0\leq a,b\leq2.
\end{equation}
Normalize the Grothendieck residue trace by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue-trace}
 \epsilon_0(f)
 =16\operatorname{Res}_0
   \frac{f\,dX\wedge dY}{(4X^3)(4Y^3)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Classical primary vertex]
\label{prop:classical-vertex}
The pairing $\eta_0(f,g)=\epsilon_0(fg)$ is nondegenerate and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical-pairing}
 \eta_0(e_{a,b},e_{c,d})
 =\delta_{a+c,2}\delta_{b+d,2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the ordinary GKT node involution is the socle channel
$e_{a,b}e_{2-a,2-b}=e_{2,2}$ with coefficient one.  In a fixed
ordered-residue chart, its leading monomials give the no-bend theta vertex.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The residue in \eqref{eq:residue-trace} extracts the coefficient of
$X^2Y^2$.  This proves \eqref{eq:classical-pairing}, nondegeneracy, and the
socle identity.  Additivity of $F$ carries the identity to leading theta
monomials.
\end{proof}

Five coefficient layers occur in the comparison.  A Jacobi wall coefficient
belongs to the logarithm of a crossing automorphism.  A raw terminal
coefficient is obtained by summing bend contributions in an ambient monomial
product.  A theta structure constant appears only after the re-expansion
\eqref{eq:theta-reexpansion}.  A potential coefficient depends on a
right-equivalence and primitive-form gauge.  A Frobenius structure constant
is obtained only after Jacobian reduction in the period-normalized
Kodaira--Spencer basis.  Numerically equal rationals in different layers are
not identified without an intertwining theorem.

There is already a unipotent ambiguity in the first mixed theta window.  If
the deck involution exchanges $\vartheta_\alpha$ and $\vartheta_\beta$ and
fixes $\vartheta_\rho$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta-ambiguity}
 \vartheta_\rho^{(\lambda)}
 =\vartheta_\rho+\lambda t_\alpha t_\beta\vartheta_0
\end{equation}
preserves the leading term, grading, deck equivariance, and framed
transport.  Unary scattering therefore does not select the invariant
product basis.  The commutator lies in the Prym summand, while
$\vartheta_\alpha\vartheta_\beta$ lies in the symmetric summand.  Periods
remove the ambiguity.

\subsection{Marginal calibration}

Set $t=t_{2,2}$.  The marginal quartics are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABC-family}
 W_{A,B,C}=AX^4+BX^2Y^2+CY^4,
 \qquad \Omega=dX\wedge dY.
\end{equation}
The volume-preserving coordinate change
$(X,Y)\mapsto(qX,q^{-1}Y)$ acts by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-cochain}
 (A,B,C)\longmapsto(q^4A,B,q^{-4}C).
\end{equation}
This is the quartic GHS zero-cochain action; its invariants are $B$ and $AC$
\cite{grossetal2016theta}.  Each orbit with $AC$ a unit has a unique
reflection-fixed representative $A=C=\sqrt{AC}$ after choosing the branch at
the Fermat point.

Let $\Xi_{0,0}$ and $\Xi_{2,2}$ be the two relevant product good-basis
cycles.  For $\alpha=A-1$ and $\beta=B$, define
\begin{align}
 F_0(\alpha,\beta)
 &=\sum_{i,j,\ell\geq0}
 \frac{(-1)^{i+j}(\frac14)_{i+\ell}(\frac14)_{j+\ell}}
      {i!j!(2\ell)!}
 \alpha^{i+j}\beta^{2\ell},\label{eq:F0}\\
 F_2(\alpha,\beta)
 &=\sum_{i,j,\ell\geq0}
 \frac{(-1)^{i+j}(\frac34)_{i+\ell}(\frac34)_{j+\ell}}
      {i!j!(2\ell+1)!}
 \alpha^{i+j}\beta^{2\ell+1}.\label{eq:F2}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Marginal period calibration]
\label{thm:marginal-calibration}
There is a unique pair $A(t),B(t)\in\QQ[[t]]$, with $A(0)=1$ and
$B(t)=t+O(t^2)$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:period-inverse}
 F_0(A(t)-1,B(t))=1,
 \qquad F_2(A(t)-1,B(t))=t.
\end{equation}
It is the reflection-fixed GKT/Saito-flat marginal potential
\[
 W_t^{\mathrm{flat}}=A(t)(X^4+Y^4)+B(t)X^2Y^2,
\]
where
\begin{align}
 A(t)&=1+\frac{t^2}{16}+\frac{t^4}{768}
       +\frac{t^6}{61440}+O(t^8),\label{eq:A-series}\\
 B(t)&=t+\frac{t^5}{1280}+O(t^9).\label{eq:B-series}
\end{align}
The two quadratic endpoint invariants satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal-invariants}
 \nu_{(4,0)}=\nu_{(0,4)}=-\frac18.
\end{equation}
The cubic marginal invariant vanishes, while the first product-visible
correction is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal-product}
 X^3=\left(-\frac t2+\frac{t^3}{32}\right)XY^2,
 \qquad
 Y^3=\left(-\frac t2+\frac{t^3}{32}\right)X^2Y
 \pmod{t^4}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The quartic good-basis moments are
\[
 P_0(4k)=(-\hbar)^k\left(\frac14\right)_k,
 \qquad
 P_2(4k+2)=(-\hbar)^k\left(\frac34\right)_k,
\]
and vanish in the complementary congruence classes.  Expanding
\[
 \exp\!\left(
  \frac{\alpha(X^4+Y^4)+\beta X^2Y^2}{\hbar}
 \right)
\]
against $\Xi_{0,0}$ and $\Xi_{2,2}$ gives precisely
\eqref{eq:F0} and \eqref{eq:F2}: an even number of central insertions
contributes to the first period and an odd number to the second.  The
primitive-form normalization is \eqref{eq:period-inverse}.

At the origin the Jacobian of $(F_0,F_2)$ with respect to $(\alpha,\beta)$ is
$\operatorname{diag}(-1/2,1)$.  The formal inverse function theorem gives
existence and uniqueness; coefficient extraction gives
\eqref{eq:A-series}--\eqref{eq:B-series}.

At order $t^2$,
\[
 F_0=1-\frac12(A-1)+\frac{B^2}{32}+O(t^3),
\]
so $A-1=t^2/16+O(t^3)$.  The sign and order-two automorphism factor in the
GKT potential multiply either reflection-related endpoint invariant by
$-1/2$.  Hence \eqref{eq:marginal-invariants}.  The coefficient of $t^3$ in
the second period equation gives the cubic vanishing.  Finally the Jacobian
relation is $X^3=-B(2A)^{-1}XY^2$, and expansion of $-B/(2A)$ gives
\eqref{eq:marginal-product}.
\end{proof}

The coefficient $1/16$ in $A(t)$ is a potential coefficient.  The
coefficient $1/32$ in \eqref{eq:marginal-product} is a Jacobian product
coefficient.  The latter is path independent when read on transported source
sections, because framed transport is an algebra isomorphism and carries the
Jacobian ideal by the chain rule.

The residue pairing detects the flat gauge already at quadratic order.  For
$W_c=(1+ct^2)(X^4+Y^4)+tX^2Y^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue-gauge}
 16\operatorname{Res}_0
 \frac{X^2Y^2\,dX\wedge dY}{\partial_XW_c\,\partial_YW_c}
 =\frac1{(1+ct^2)^2-t^2/4}.
\end{equation}
Thus the primitive form distinguishes $c=1/16$ before ordinary
multiplication does.

\subsection{Brieskorn transport}

Let $R$ be a finite labelled coefficient quotient and $\mathcal P$ the
polynomial or finite root-valued theta-coordinate algebra in a path chart.
For $W\in\mathcal P$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Brieskorn-module}
 \operatorname{GM}_\hbar(W)
 =H^2\!\left(
  \Omega_{\mathcal P/R}^{\bullet}[[\hbar]],
  \hbar d+dW\wedge
 \right).
\end{equation}
This is a scalar complex in a chosen path chart.  The complexes in different
charts are glued by coordinate pullback.  The distinguished primitive-form
classes glue at the same time with the transition of $\Lcal_\kappa$; we do
not identify the scalar de Rham complexes by tensoring their differentials
with a nontrivial line bundle.
The quartic annular path group is generated infinitesimally by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic-vector-field}
 \mathcal X_{a,b}
 =X^aY^b\bigl((b+1)X\partial_X-(a+1)Y\partial_Y\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its divergence with respect to $dX\wedge dY$ is zero.

\begin{proposition}[Framed Brieskorn transport]
\label{prop:Brieskorn-transport}
Let $P_\Gamma$ be the total coordinate transport along a framed annular path
represented at a finite stage.  Pullback gives an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Brieskorn-transport}
 \operatorname{GM}_\hbar(W)
 \xrightarrow{\sim}
 \operatorname{GM}_\hbar(P_\Gamma W),
\end{equation}
carrying the primitive form to
$dP_\Gamma(X)\wedge dP_\Gamma(Y)$, with the line multiplier across slabs.
These maps compose strictly and commute with coefficient and root
restriction, residual corrections, tree associators, and changes of framed paths.
Modulo $\hbar$ they preserve the Jacobian algebra and normalized residue
pairing; dually they preserve oscillatory periods after inverse transport of
cycles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every coordinate automorphism $\phi$ and differential form $\xi$,
\[
 \bigl(\hbar d+d(\phi^*W)\wedge\bigr)\phi^*\xi
 =\phi^*\bigl((\hbar d+dW\wedge)\xi\bigr).
\]
Thus pullback is a chain isomorphism.  The flows of
\eqref{eq:quartic-vector-field} preserve $dX\wedge dY$.  Across a slab,
Equation~\eqref{eq:line-transition} supplies the inverse conformal
multiplier and preserves the line-valued primitive form.  Composition and
finite-stage compatibility follow from functoriality.  Reduction modulo
$\hbar$ gives the Koszul complex of $dW$, and change of variables with the
primitive form preserves its Grothendieck residue.
\end{proof}

If a total wall factor is written as a GKT factor followed by its residual
factor, the latter therefore acts as a volume-preserving right equivalence of
potential-with-form pairs.  It is retained as a coordinate change, not
replaced by a scalar.  The same observation turns the residual associativity
and framed-path cocycles into ordinary composition identities of Brieskorn
chain maps.

\subsection{The simultaneous primary unfolding}

Let $W^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}\in A_{\mathrm{prim}}[[X,Y]]$ be the
coordinate-symmetric primary GKT potential after all descendent variables
are set to zero.  It comes with the nine good-basis cycles
$\Xi_{a,b}$ and the primitive form $dX\wedge dY$.  The GKT mirror theorem
states simultaneously that the nine parameters $t_{a,b}$ are Saito-flat
coordinates for this unfolding
\cite[Thm.~0.3 and Cor.~4.17]{grossetal2022open}; see also
\cite{lietal2017,heetal2022}.

\begin{theorem}[Transport of the GKT primary Saito structure]
\label{thm:primary-Saito-system}
Transport $W^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}$, its primitive form, and its nine
good-basis cycles along any finite annular path frame.  The transported
primitive-form class remains primitive, and all nine $t_{a,b}$ remain
simultaneous Saito-flat coordinates.  These transports compose under path
concatenation and commute with all finite coefficient, root, residual, tree,
and framed-path maps.  After narrow restriction, the degree-two symmetric
pushforward gives a framed narrow Saito local system.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:Brieskorn-transport} preserves every oscillatory
period after inverse transport of the cycles.  Hence the primitive-form and
flat-coordinate identities in the source frame hold in every transported
frame, simultaneously in all nine variables.  Strict composition gives the
cocycle statements.  The primary narrow variables are stable under the
degree-two deck action; exactness of the Reynolds idempotent in characteristic
zero then gives the symmetric local system.
\end{proof}

The first two-dimensional slice already tests simultaneity.  Set
$u=t_{1,1}$ and $t=t_{2,2}$.  Through total degree three,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-primary-potential}
 W_{u,t}
 =\left(1+\frac{t^2}{16}\right)(X^4+Y^4)
  +tX^2Y^2
  +\left(u-\frac{ut^2}{16}\right)XY
  -\frac{u^2t}{32}+O((u,t)^4).
\end{equation}
The two new labelled block invariants are
\[
 \nu_{ut^2;(1,1)}=-\frac18,
 \qquad
 \nu_{u^2t;(0,0)}=-\frac1{16}.
\]
The repeated-marking automorphisms divide these by two, giving the last two
corrections in \eqref{eq:mixed-primary-potential}.  For the $\Xi_{1,1}$
period, the direct correction, its quadratic cross term, and the cubic
singleton term contribute
\[
 -\frac{ut^2}{16\hbar},\qquad
 -\frac{ut^2}{16\hbar},\qquad
 \frac{ut^2}{8\hbar};
\]
for $\Xi_{0,0}$ the two contributions are
$-u^2t/(32\hbar)$ and $u^2t/(32\hbar)$.  Both sums vanish.  Thus the constant
term $-u^2t/32$ is fixed by the oscillatory integral although it is invisible
to the critical locus.

\subsection{Kodaira--Spencer classes in theta coordinates}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-primary-Jacobian}
 \mathcal P_0=A_{\mathrm{prim}}[[X,Y]],
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_0=\mathcal P_0/
  (\partial_XW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}},
   \partial_YW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}).
\end{equation}
For a framed path $\Gamma$, write
\[
 U_\Gamma=P_\Gamma(X),
 \qquad V_\Gamma=P_\Gamma(Y),
 \qquad W_\Gamma=P_\Gamma(W^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}})
\]
and define the Jacobian quotient in the transported coordinate algebra
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transported-primary-Jacobian}
 \mathcal R_\Gamma
 =P_\Gamma(\mathcal P_0)/
  (\partial_{U_\Gamma}W_\Gamma,
   \partial_{V_\Gamma}W_\Gamma).
\end{equation}
The transported period-normalized Kodaira--Spencer classes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-sections}
 \Phi_{a,b}^\Gamma
 =P_\Gamma\!\left(
   \left[\frac{\partial W^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}}
              {\partial t_{a,b}}\right]\right)
 \in\mathcal R_\Gamma,
 \qquad 0\leq a,b\leq2.
\end{equation}
Here ``in theta coordinates'' means that the classes are expressed in the
framed theta-coordinate algebra; they need not be individual canonical theta
functions.

\begin{theorem}[Transported narrow-primary Frobenius structure]
\label{thm:primary-Frobenius}
The nine sections \eqref{eq:KS-sections} form an
$A_{\mathrm{prim}}$-basis of $\mathcal R_\Gamma$ and satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KS-naturality}
 \Phi_{a,b}^\Gamma=P_\Gamma(\Phi_{a,b}^0),
 \qquad
 \Phi_{a,b}^0=X^aY^b+O(\mathfrak m_{\mathrm{prim}}).
\end{equation}
Their multiplication, unit $\Phi_{0,0}=[1]$, and normalized residue pairing
have coefficient matrices independent of $\Gamma$.  They are the complete
primary Saito--Givental Frobenius structure transported from
$W^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}$.  By the GKT primary mirror theorem, the source
tensor is the narrow-primary closed FJRW Frobenius structure.

Constructing the product equivariantly on the labelled cover, restricting to
the narrow primary subobject, and then applying $e_+$ gives an isomorphism of
framed narrow-primary Frobenius algebras.  This construction is compatible
with every finite coefficient and Kummer presentation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The chain rule gives
\[
 P_\Gamma(\partial_XW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}})
 =\partial_{U_\Gamma}W_\Gamma,
 \qquad
 P_\Gamma(\partial_YW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}})
 =\partial_{V_\Gamma}W_\Gamma.
\]
Hence $P_\Gamma$ identifies $\mathcal R_0$ with $\mathcal R_\Gamma$.
Although $P_\Gamma$ fixes the coefficient ring, its coordinate expressions
may depend on the parameters.  For
$\partial_i=\partial/\partial t_{a,b}$, put
\[
 \xi_i=(\partial_i\circ P_\Gamma-P_\Gamma\circ\partial_i)
        P_\Gamma^{-1}.
\]
Thus $\xi_i$ is the coefficient-linear infinitesimal variation of the
coordinate map.  It is a derivation of the transported coordinate algebra,
and
\[
 \partial_iW_\Gamma
 =P_\Gamma(\partial_iW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}})+\xi_i(W_\Gamma).
\]
Writing $\xi_i=A_i\partial_{U_\Gamma}+B_i\partial_{V_\Gamma}$ shows that the
second term lies in the Jacobian ideal.  Consequently
\[
 \left[\partial_iW_\Gamma\right]
 =P_\Gamma\!\left([\partial_iW^{\GKT}_{\mathrm{prim}}]\right)
 \quad\text{in }\mathcal R_\Gamma.
\]
Equivalently, \eqref{eq:KS-sections} uses the connection obtained by
transporting the trivial coefficient connection from the source frame.  This
proves naturality even for parameter-dependent path maps.

The primary GKT potential is a formal miniversal unfolding, so its critical
algebra is finite free of rank nine.  At the Fermat point the derivatives in
\eqref{eq:KS-sections} are the nine monomials in
\eqref{eq:Fermat-Jacobian}; their change-of-basis determinant is therefore a
unit.  This proves the basis assertion.

Since $t_{0,0}$ is the constant deformation in the source potential,
$\Phi_{0,0}^0=[1]$ and hence $\Phi_{0,0}^\Gamma=[1]$ in every frame.

The normalized residue pairing is nondegenerate at the Fermat point by
Proposition~\ref{prop:classical-vertex} and remains so over the complete local
base.  Its trace identity
\[
 \eta(fg,h)=\epsilon(fgh)=\eta(f,gh)
\]
makes the transported critical algebra Frobenius.  Theorem
\ref{thm:primary-Saito-system} identifies the $t_{a,b}$ as simultaneous flat
coordinates, so the Kodaira--Spencer product is the Saito Frobenius product.
The GKT primary mirror theorem
\cite[Thm.~0.3]{grossetal2022open} identifies the source tensor with the
narrow-primary closed FJRW tensor.  Path independence follows by applying the algebra isomorphism
$P_\Gamma$ to the source product and comparing coefficients in the
transported basis.

The deck involution preserves the potential and primitive form.  Its
symmetric and Prym summands are therefore orthogonal for the residue metric;
nondegeneracy of the full metric implies nondegeneracy on each summand.  Deck
equivariance makes the symmetric summand a unital subalgebra.  The idempotent
$e_+$ commutes with path transport and all finite restrictions, completing
the descent statement.
\end{proof}

The theorem compares the complete primary structure.  It includes all nine
deformation directions, flat
coordinates, multiplication, unit, and residue metric.  It does not identify
an unquotiented pair-of-broken-lines coefficient with an individual open
descendent invariant.  On the primary slice there are no positive-weight
critical walls: the source theta product is ordinary multiplication, and
nontrivial coefficients such as $1/32$ appear only after reduction of an
out-of-range monomial in the period-normalized Jacobian quotient.

Thus the annular geometry changes the presentation of the Frobenius algebra
but not its tensor.  Hyperbolic holonomy remains, so the algebras form a
path-equivariant local system rather than an unframed global theta basis.
