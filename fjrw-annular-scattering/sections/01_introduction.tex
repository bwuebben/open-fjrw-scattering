\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{The question}

The Gross--Siebert program constructs mirrors by placing wall-crossing
automorphisms on an integral-affine base with singularities and reading off
broken lines and theta functions
\cite{grosshackingkeel2015,grossetal2018canonical,grosssiebert2022}.  Its
input is geometric: a base, its singular locus, a polyhedral decomposition,
and a polarization.  This paper asks whether the wall-crossing of an open
Landau--Ginzburg theory supplies such a base, and answers the question for
one model.

For $x^4+y^4$ the answer is partial.  Under the matching of $x^4$ and $y^4$
with the rays of the coordinate divisors $\{X=0\}$ and $\{Y=0\}$ of
$\PP(1,1,2)$, the exponents of the wall-crossing generators attached to one
internal marking, together with the integral exponents of the base of Carl,
Pumperla and Siebert, determine an integral-affine
annulus, a double cover of that base branched at two of its three singular
points, and the annulus carries a wall structure that is consistent at its
two joints, in a sense weaker than that of Gross and Siebert.  The
wall-crossing of this structure agrees with the transition of Gross, Kelly
and Tessler between their two extremal boundary conditions (defined below)
for one internal marking of twist $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$ and descendent degree
one, and differs from it for one such internal marking of each of these
twists, by a factor that does not change periods
(Theorems~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} and~\ref{thm:intro-comparison} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-exact}).

Gross, Kelly, and Tessler (GKT) define genus-zero open FJRW invariants for
$(W,G)=(x^r+y^s,\mu_r\times\mu_s)$ as relative Euler numbers on moduli spaces
of $W$-spin disks \cite{grossetal2022open}.  The invariants depend on a choice
of canonical boundary conditions, and changes of boundary conditions act
through a formal wall-crossing group closely related to the tropical vertex
group.  This action has the algebra of a scattering diagram but no
integral-affine realization: no affine manifold, singular locus, or
polyhedral decomposition carries its walls.  Gross, Kelly, and Tessler list a
tropical version of open FJRW theory among the open problems of their survey
\cite[\S6]{grossetal2026survey}, and Maher observes that it is not obvious
how to incorporate Landau--Ginzburg mirror symmetry into the Gross--Siebert
program, since a Landau--Ginzburg model has no target space and hence no SYZ
fibration \cite[Rem.~5.1.1]{maher2024predictions}.

In algebraic terms, the theory for $W=x^4+y^4$ reads as follows
(Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections} recalls the definitions).  The
internal markings of a disk carry distinct \emph{labels}, each with a twist,
and we refer to an internal marking by its label.  Each internal marking
also carries a descendent degree $d\geq0$, and a label $i$ contributes the
nilpotent deformation variables $u_{i,d}$, $d\geq0$, any two of which have
product zero; we write $t_i=u_{i,1}$, so that $t_i^2=0$, and say that a
label has descendent degree one when only its variable $t_i$ is used.
Boundary markings are of three kinds, of twists $(2,0)$, $(0,2)$ and
$(2,2)$.  A choice of canonical boundary conditions chooses the
multisections of the descendent Witten bundles whose weighted zero counts
are the open invariants.  The invariants appear, with signs, as the
coefficients of the \emph{potential}
$\sum_\Gamma\pm\nu_\Gamma\,x^{k_1}y^{k_2}u_\Gamma$, a deformation of
$x^4+y^4$.  Its monomials correspond to the \emph{balanced} disk graphs
$\Gamma$, those with one boundary marking of the third kind on whose moduli
space the rank of the descendent Witten bundle equals the dimension;
$\nu_\Gamma$ is the invariant of $\Gamma$, $k_1$ and $k_2$ are its numbers of
boundary markings of the first two kinds, and $u_\Gamma$ is the product of
the variables $u_{i,d}$ of its labels $i$ and their descendent degrees $d$;
the two balanced graphs without labels give $x^4+y^4$.  The wall-crossing
group is generated, with coefficients in these variables, by the
Hamiltonian vector fields $X_k$ of the monomials $x^{k_1+1}y^{k_2+1}$ for
$dx\wedge dy$, one for each \emph{critical} graph, which has $k_1+1$ and
$k_2+1$ boundary markings of the first two kinds, none of the third, and a
descendent Witten bundle of rank one more than the dimension.  These fields are $(xy)^{-1}$ times the generators
$x^{m_1}y^{m_2}\partial_n$, $\langle m,n\rangle=0$, with
$\partial_n=n_1x\partial_x+n_2y\partial_y$, of the Lie algebra of the
tropical vertex group of \cite{grosspandharipandesiebert2010}
(Section~\ref{subsec:volume-line}).

The companion paper \cite{wuebben2026boundary} factors the GKT
wall-crossing into factors ordered by slope.
The \emph{boundary ray} of the generator $X_k$ is the ray spanned by the
numbers $k+(1,1)$ of boundary markings of the first two kinds on its
critical graph.  This is a ray in the plane of marking counts, not in any affine
base.  GKT characterize the vectors of invariants that boundary conditions
can produce by explicit equations, the chamber conditions; their solutions,
the \emph{chamber indices}, are exactly the vectors of open invariants of
transverse canonical boundary conditions
(Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}).  For a nonempty set of labels and
a descendent vector on it, the coefficients of the potential at the balanced
graphs of these data form their \emph{balanced string}, indexed by the
exponents of the corresponding monomials; these exponents form an arithmetic
progression with step $(4,-4)$.  The two \emph{extremal} chamber indices are those for
which, on every balanced string, only the coefficient with the least,
respectively greatest, first exponent survives; we call their potentials
the \emph{extremal GKT potentials} $W^{\min}$ and $W^{\max}$, and canonical
boundary conditions realizing them \emph{extremal boundary conditions}.
The \emph{GKT transition} between the two extremal chamber indices is the
element of the wall-crossing group that carries $W^{\min}$ to $W^{\max}$
(Section~\ref{subsec:labelled-critical}).  Modulo any finite set of
deformation monomials closed under taking divisors, it factors uniquely into
factors ordered by the slope of their boundary rays
\cite[Thm.~1.1 and Prop.~3.1]{wuebben2026boundary}.  The factorization supplies
an ordered family of wall-crossing operations, but no surface on which their
rays lie and no affine monodromy.

We construct such a surface, and a wall structure on it that is consistent at
its two joints, for the
parabolic quartic $W=x^4+y^4$, the singularity $X_9$ (also denoted
$\widetilde E_7$).  Its central charge
$\widehat c=2-\frac2r-\frac2s$ equals one; by \cite{wuebben2026threshold} the
primary invariants are independent of the boundary conditions exactly when
$\widehat c<1$.  The suspension of the marginal pencil of $X_9$ is an
anticanonical elliptic curve in $\PP(1,1,2)$, and the toric-boundary
degeneration of that pair gives the disk-shaped intersection complex of the
Landau--Ginzburg construction of Carl--Pumperla--Siebert \cite{carletal2024},
the \emph{CPS complex}.  It is the natural candidate base: the
anticanonical polygon of $\PP(1,1,2)$, subdivided by the three segments from
its interior lattice point, the \emph{central vertex}, to its vertices, each
segment carrying one singular point of the affine structure
(Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover}).

Independently of the annulus, Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration}
normalizes the GKT primary potential of $x^4+y^4$.  For two labels of twist
$(2,2)$ it gives open invariants whose sum is $-\frac14$, in agreement with
the period computation of \cite[Rem.~4.3]{wuebben2026boundary}; this is the
sum considered in \cite[Ex.~5.5]{grossetal2026survey}, where the value
$-\frac18$ is printed (Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-calibration}).

\subsection{Notation}\label{subsec:intro-notation}

Appendix~\ref{app:notation} lists the principal symbols of the paper and
compares the lattice conventions with those of toric geometry and of
\cite{grosssiebert2011,carletal2024}.
Let $M\simeq\ZZ^2$ be the lattice of the fan of $\PP(1,1,2)$.  In toric
geometry this lattice is called $N$; we write $M$ for it because it carries
the exponents of all Laurent monomials, slab functions and wall functions
below, as $M$ does in \cite{grosspandharipandesiebert2010}.  The polygon of
the CPS complex lies in $M^\vee\otimes\RR$, with
$M^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\ZZ)$.  The discrete Legendre transform of
the CPS complex is its \emph{dual intersection complex} in the sense of
\cite[\S7.1]{carletal2024}; its bounded cell is the polar polygon in
$M\otimes\RR$, and its tangent lattice is $M$
(Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover}).  Let
$\ZZ^2_{x,y}$ be the lattice of bidegrees $(a,b)$ of monomials $x^ay^b$, and
put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-F}
 F=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \widetilde M=F(\ZZ^2_{x,y}),
 \qquad \widetilde M/M\simeq\ZZ/2\oplus\ZZ/4.
\end{equation}
The \emph{exponent map} $F$ sends $x^ay^b$ to the character $z^{F(a,b)}$,
whose exponent has coordinates $(a,b)$ in the basis $F(1,0),F(0,1)$ of
$\widetilde M$, its \emph{$F$-coordinates}.  It
is the unique linear map that matches $x^4$ and $y^4$ with the rays of the
coordinate divisors $\{X=0\}$ and $\{Y=0\}$ of $\PP(1,1,2)$
(Section~\ref{subsec:exponent-lattices}).  The CPS complex has three
singular points $A,B,C$ (Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover}).  The slab
function attached to $B$ is $1+a_\rho z^u+z^{2u}$, with $z^u$ a primitive
character and a free coefficient $a_\rho$, the \emph{slab parameter}; at the
\emph{nodal values} $a_\rho=\pm2$ it is a square, and the associated
anticanonical curve acquires a node (Section~\ref{sec:affine-kummer}).

For $q=(q_1,q_2)\in\ZZ^2_{x,y}$, the derivation
\[
 X^\kappa_q=x^{q_1-1}y^{q_2-1}\bigl(q_2\,x\partial_x-q_1\,y\partial_y\bigr)
\]
is the Hamiltonian vector field of the function $x^{q_1}y^{q_2}$ for
$dx\wedge dy$; the
superscript records the shift of the exponent by $\kappa=(1,1)$, the exponent
of $xy$, and $F$ carries $X^\kappa_q$ to a monomial derivation of the
character ring of $\widetilde M$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:integral-cofactor}).
The \emph{cofactor} $L=-\frac12F^{-T}$ of \eqref{eq:cofactor}, a linear map
from bidegrees to $M^\vee\otimes\RR$ that intertwines the exchange of $x$
and $y$ on bidegrees with its action on the plane of the polygon, gives the
direction $Lq$ in that plane along which the wall of $X^\kappa_q$ is placed
(Section~\ref{subsec:exponent-lattices}).  We write $X_{a,b}=X^\kappa_{\kappa+(a,b)}$ and
$E_{a,b}=t_1^at_2^bX_{a,b}$, so that $\mathsf A=-\frac12E_{1,0}$ and
$\mathsf B=-\frac12E_{0,1}$.  The sans-serif letters $\mathsf A$ and
$\mathsf B$ always denote these two Hamiltonian vector fields; outside the
GKT notation of Section~\ref{sec:gkt-decoration} and
Appendices~\ref{app:gkt-completion} and~\ref{app:primary-calibration}, the
roman letters $A,B,C$, also with decorations such as $\widetilde A$ or $B_0$
and as indices, refer to singular points of the affine structure, apart from
the ring $A$ of the root-stack convention of Appendix~\ref{app:calculations},
the logarithms $C_{\mathfrak r}$ of ray factors and the corrections $C_i$ of
Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence}.  The
\emph{singleton parameters} $t_1,t_2$ are
the variables $t_i$ of a single label of twist $(1,0)$, respectively
$(0,1)$, and descendent degree one.  The \emph{cuts} are
six arcs, from the four singular points of the annulus of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} to its boundary, that carry the
transitions between charts (Section~\ref{subsec:kummer-charts}).  Their
complement in the interior of the annulus consists of two simply connected
\emph{sheets} $S_0$ and $S_1$, which contain the two lifts $O_0$ and $O_1$
of the central vertex, respectively.  Over a simply connected region of the
annulus away from its singular points, a \emph{frame} is a trivialization of the local
system $\widetilde M$, that is, an identification of the algebra of
characters over the region with $\Bbbk[\widetilde M]$; the frame of $S_0$ is
the one induced by the chart pulled back from the CPS complex, and the
other frames are transported from it across the cuts
(Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover}).  The monodromy of a local system of
lattices along a loop is also called its holonomy, and we use the two words
interchangeably; the \emph{affine holonomy} of a loop is the affine map by
which the developing map changes along it.  A
\emph{Jacobi bracket} on a line bundle is a Lie bracket on its local
sections that is a first-order bidifferential operator, a local Lie algebra
with one-dimensional fibers in the sense of Kirillov
\cite{kirillov1976,lichnerowicz1978}; the notion is unrelated to the
Jacobian algebra $\Jac(W)$ of a potential.

\subsection{Main results}

The generators of the GKT critical Lie algebra of $x^4+y^4$ attached to a
single label of twist $(1,0)$, respectively $(0,1)$, and descendent degree
one are, in the notation of Section~\ref{subsec:intro-notation},
\[
 \mathsf A=-\tfrac12t_1X^\kappa_{(2,1)},
 \qquad
 \mathsf B=-\tfrac12t_2X^\kappa_{(1,2)},
\]
the logarithms of the two singleton factors of the GKT transition between
the extremal chamber indices (Section~\ref{subsec:first-mixed-ray}).  Their
exponents, with standard coordinates
$F(2,1)=(\frac14,-\frac12)$ and $F(1,2)=(-\frac14,-1)$ in $M\otimes\QQ$,
are fractional: they do not lie in $M$, and together with $M$ they span the
overlattice $\widetilde M$ of index eight.

Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} uses the terms \emph{wall structure}
and \emph{consistency} in a weaker sense than in the Gross--Siebert
program.  The walls of $\mathfrak D$, the wall structure of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}(4), are not tangent to the exponents
of their functions, as they are in
\cite{grosspandharipandesiebert2010,grossetal2016theta}: the exponents are tangent
vectors of the dual intersection complex, and the supports lie in the plane
of the polygon.
Tangency transported by the discrete Legendre transform would place each wall
along the annihilator of its exponent, a rule incompatible with the exchange
of $x$ and $y$; the compatible linear rules are the gradient maps of
exchange-invariant quadratic functions, and the supports are placed by one
of them, the cofactor $L$, chosen by convention
(Section~\ref{subsec:exponent-lattices}).  Consistency is the local
condition around the two joints $O_0$ and $O_1$, the lifts of the central
vertex.  It is weaker than the consistency of a wall structure in the sense
of Gross and Siebert \cite{grosssiebert2011}, which is required at every
joint, every codimension-two cell of the decomposition cut out by the walls
and slabs that does not lie in the boundary of the base, including the
interior endpoints of walls
\cite[Defs.~2.27--2.28]{grosssiebert2011}: here the diagonal walls end at
the singular points $B_0$ and $B_1$ with no condition there, and, as in
\cite{grosssiebert2011}, nothing is imposed along the core loop.

\begin{maintheorem}[Annular base and wall structure]
\label{thm:intro-reconstruction}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The smallest cover of the CPS complex on which the affine monodromy
preserves $\widetilde M$ is the double cover branched at two of its three
singular points.
It is an integral-affine annulus $\overline\Acal$ with integral
tangent lattice $\widetilde M^\vee$, so that exponents lie in the dual
lattice $\widetilde M$, with two focus--focus points $B_0,B_1$, and two
ramification points $\widetilde A,\widetilde C$ of the cover at which the
structure is the pullback of a focus--focus germ under $z\mapsto z^2$; the
pullback of the polarization of the discrete Legendre transform of the CPS
complex becomes integral on it after multiplication by four
(Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-cover}).  Its holonomy $K$ along the core loop
$\gamma_{\mathrm{core}}$, which separates $\{\widetilde A,B_0\}$ from
$\{B_1,\widetilde C\}$, is hyperbolic of trace $18$, and its monodromies around the two boundary circles are conjugate to
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-4\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-16\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
\item Lifting the slab transitions requires roots of the slab functions, of
degrees $2,4,4,2$ at $\widetilde A,B_0,B_1,\widetilde C$ (the degree at
$B_0,B_1$ drops to two at the nodal values).  The roots of the slab
functions transported along all paths generate an infinite Kummer extension:
no finite Kummer extension containing the initial roots is stable under
transport, and the system of all transported roots is not represented by a
finite root stack.  The functions transported along any finite set of paths
lie in a single finite Kummer extension (Theorems~\ref{thm:infinite-root-orbit}
and~\ref{thm:forced-category}).
\item The \emph{lifted slab maps}, which multiply each character $z^m$,
$m\in\widetilde M$, by an integral power of a root of the slab function as
in part~(2) and do not change its exponent, rescale the volume form
$dx\wedge dy$ by the
inverse of the slab function at $\widetilde A$ and $\widetilde C$, by the
inverse of a square root of it at $B_0$, and by a square root of it at
$B_1$, and the transitions carried by the cuts do not preserve
$dx\wedge dy$, so its Poisson bivector cannot serve in every chart
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-scalar-frame}).  The volume form glues as a form
with values in a line bundle $\Lcal_\kappa$ over the covers obtained by
adjoining the roots of part~(2), and its Poisson bracket, transported along paths
from the frame of the sheet $S_0$, is a Jacobi bracket on $\Lcal_\kappa$ whose bivector
part, with respect to the transported local generators of $\Lcal_\kappa$, is
the bivector of $dx\wedge dy$ in the frame transported to every vertex of the path tree,
the tree of reduced sequences of crossings of the cuts that start in
$S_0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:Jacobi-transport}).
\item Place $e^{\mathsf A}$ and $e^{\mathsf B}$ on the two lines through a lift $O_0$
of the central vertex in the directions $L(2,1)$ and $L(1,2)$.  On the ray
from $O_0$ in direction $Lq$, place the inverse of the factor on the ray of
$q$ in the ordered factorization of the group commutator
$e^{\mathsf A}e^{-\mathsf B}e^{-\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}$, and transport the
result to the other lift $O_1$.  This gives a wall structure $\mathfrak D$
on the complement $\Acal^\circ$ of the singular points that, at every finite
order, is locally finite, has properly embedded arcs as supports, and is
consistent at $O_0$ and $O_1$, wall structures and consistency being taken
in the weaker sense of Definition~\ref{def:Jacobi-wall} and the paragraph
after it (Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-supports} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency}).
\item The holonomy $K$ acts hyperbolically on $\widetilde M$, with no
nonzero finite orbit.  Consequently the local system $\widetilde M$ on
$\Acal^\circ$ has no nonzero global section, and neither does its pullback
to any connected finite cover of $\Acal^\circ$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:theta-local-system}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{maintheorem}

In part~(4), consistency at the joints depends on the placement only through
the cyclic order of the supports about $O_0$, which is the reverse of the
cyclic order of their exponents in $F$-coordinates ($L$ reverses
orientation), an exponent $q$ being replaced by $-q$ on the negative halves
of the two lines; since $F$ also reverses orientation, this is the same
cyclic order as that of the characters $F(q)$ in the standard coordinates of
$M\otimes\RR$ (Section~\ref{subsec:exponent-lattices}).

Part~(1) rests on the monodromies of the CPS complex about its three
singular points.  In the basis $F(1,0),F(0,1)$ of $\widetilde M$, the
monodromy about $B$ is an integral matrix, whereas those about $A$ and $C$
have half-integral entries and therefore do not preserve $\widetilde M$
\eqref{eq:unbranched-conjugates}.  Let $\chi$ be the character of the
fundamental group of the disk punctured at $A,B,C$ with values in $\ZZ/2$
that sends the loops about $A$ and $C$ to $1$ and the loop about $B$ to $0$.
The monodromies of a basis of its kernel are integral in this basis
(Appendix~\ref{app:calculations}), so the loops whose monodromy preserves
$\widetilde M$ form a proper subgroup containing the kernel, which has index
two; the two subgroups therefore coincide, and the smallest cover is the
double cover classified by $\chi$, the \emph{parity cover}.  It is branched
at $A$ and $C$, the point $B$ has two unramified lifts $B_0,B_1$, and the
boundary loop, on which $\chi$ takes the value $1+0+1=0$, has two lifts.  By Riemann--Hurwitz the
cover has Euler characteristic $2\cdot1-2=0$, so it is an annulus with two
boundary circles.  With respect to $\widetilde M$ the monodromies about
$B_0$ and $B_1$ are primitive unipotent, and these points are primitive
focus--focus singularities, whereas a small loop about $\widetilde A$ or
$\widetilde C$ develops onto a curve winding twice around the developed
singular point (Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-cover}).

Part~(2) rests on one computation.  Transport about $\widetilde A$ fixes the
invariant character $x_A$ of $\widetilde A$ and multiplies the invariant
character $x_B$ of $B_0$ by the nonconstant function $(1+x_A^{-1})^4$, so
the transported slab functions $f_B\bigl((1+x_A^{-1})^{4n}x_B\bigr)$,
$n\in\ZZ$, with $f_B(x)=1+a_\rho x+x^2$, are pairwise coprime, each with a
simple prime factor that divides no other (after extracting the square at
the nodal values), and their fourth roots generate an infinite Kummer
extension (Section~\ref{subsec:root-orbit}).

In part~(3), a divergence computation shows that conjugation by a lifted
slab map gives each singleton field a nonzero divergence for $dx\wedge dy$,
so that it is no longer Hamiltonian
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-scalar-frame}); the bracket is instead
transported along the path tree of Section~\ref{subsec:root-orbit}, the tree of reduced sequences of crossings
of the cuts that start in the sheet $S_0$.  Every vertex of a
tree is reached from its root by a unique route, so the transport is
unambiguous, and at each crossing the change of local generator of $\Lcal_\kappa$
compensates the factor by which the transition rescales $dx\wedge dy$, so
that the bivector part remains that of $dx\wedge dy$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:Jacobi-transport}).  In part~(4), beyond the
Kontsevich--Soibelman factorization at a point, the content of $\mathfrak D$
lies in the annular cover that carries it, the cyclic order of the cofactor
directions, and the fact that the walls avoid the cuts
(Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-supports}); consistency at $O_1$ follows from consistency at $O_0$: the deck involution of the cover carries the supports through $O_0$, with their crossing order and coorientations, onto those through $O_1$, and the factors there are the images of the factors at $O_0$ under one Jacobi isomorphism (Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency}).

Part~(5) rests on the trace.  In the basis $F(1,0),F(0,1)$ of
$\widetilde M$, the holonomy along the core loop $\gamma_{\mathrm{core}}$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-K}
 K=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-10&19\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}K=18.
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues $9\pm4\sqrt5$ are not roots of unity, so no power $K^r$
with $r\neq0$ fixes a nonzero element of $\widetilde M$, whereas a global
section of $\widetilde M$ over a connected finite cover of $\Acal^\circ$
would be fixed by such a power (Section~\ref{subsec:core-holonomy}).

As recalled above, each GKT generator $X_k$ is the Hamiltonian field of
$x^{k_1+1}y^{k_2+1}$ for $dx\wedge dy$; in the notation of
Section~\ref{subsec:intro-notation} it is $X^\kappa_{k+\kappa}$, indexed by
the shift $k+\kappa$ of $k$, so the GKT critical Lie algebra of every finite set of labels embeds, without change
of coefficients, in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_\kappa$ of Hamiltonian
derivations for the Jacobi bracket (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-embedding} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-operations}).  The comparison of $\mathfrak D$
with GKT wall-crossing is then as follows.

\begin{maintheorem}[Comparison with GKT wall-crossing]
\label{thm:intro-comparison}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The wall-crossing transformations of $\mathfrak D$ are not those of
the GKT transition.  For two labels
of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and descendent degree one, the \emph{first mixed
ray} $\RR_{\geq0}(1,1)$, the boundary ray of the generator $X_{1,1}$ attached
to both labels, has coefficient $-3/4$ in $\mathfrak D$
and $1/4$ in the slope-ordered factorization of the GKT transition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:first-decoration}), and the path-ordered product of
$\mathfrak D$ along a path passing the joint carries $W^{\min}$ to
$\exp(-t_1t_2X_{1,1})\,W^{\max}$ rather than to $W^{\max}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport}).
\item Fix a finite set of labels of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and
descendent degree one, let $J$ be a subset consisting of $p_J$ labels of
twist $(1,0)$ and $q_J$ of twist $(0,1)$, and put
$t_J=\prod_{i\in J}t_i$.  After the base change sending $t_1$
and $t_2$ to the sums of the variables $t_i$ of the labels of twist $(1,0)$
and $(0,1)$, respectively, the $t_J$-component of the logarithms of the wall factors of $\mathfrak D$ is
\[
 S_J=p_J!\,q_J!\,a_{p_J,q_J}\,t_JX_{p_J,q_J},
\]
where $a_{p,q}$ is the coefficient of $E_{p,q}$ in the factorization of
$e^{\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}$ into ray factors in which the factor of least
slope acts first ($a_{p,q}=-c_{p,q}$ for $p,q\geq1$, with
$c_{p,q}$ in Table~\ref{tab:singleton-coefficients}).  It agrees with the
$t_J$-component of the logarithms of the factors of the GKT transition
between the extremal chamber indices when $|J|=1$, and not when $J$
consists of one label of each twist, where it is $-\frac34t_JX_{1,1}$ and
that of the GKT transition is $\frac14t_JX_{1,1}$
(Proposition~\ref{thm:all-order-decoration}).
\item There exist an increasing sequence of finite assembling-closed systems
of labels, divisor-closed sets of deformation monomials and graph data that
exhausts all graph data;
extremal boundary conditions compatible with these systems; and a homotopy
of canonical multisections between them that is smooth on each graph datum,
with the following properties.  Here a graph datum is a graph together with
descendent degrees of its labels, a set of monomials is divisor-closed if it
contains every divisor of each of its elements, and a system is
assembling-closed if it satisfies the conditions of
Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}, among them closure under the
assembling rule of GKT described after the theorem.  The countably many ray
factors occur in
pairwise disjoint open time intervals, ordered by increasing slope.  On each
graph datum the homotopy is nonconstant on only finitely many of these
intervals and is constant near the ends of each of them.  On each of the
systems, modulo the monomials outside it, only finitely many rays carry a
nontrivial factor of the GKT transition between the extremal chamber
indices, and the potential at the right end of the interval of each ray is
the image of the potential at its left end under the factor of the GKT
transition on that ray.  The restrictions to the systems are compatible, and
the boundary conditions and the homotopy are their inverse limits.  Thus the
homotopy realizes the GKT transition factor by factor in increasing slope
(Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization} and
Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{maintheorem}

The two coefficients in part~(1) have different origins.  The coefficient
$-\frac34$ is forced by consistency at the joint: modulo $t_1^2$ and $t_2^2$
the only other walls through $O_0$ lie on the two lines carrying
$e^{\mathsf A}$ and $e^{\mathsf B}$, so the wall of the first mixed ray, the
diagonal segment from $O_0$ to $B_0$, must carry
$e^{-\mathsf B}e^{\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}e^{-\mathsf A}=\exp([\mathsf A,\mathsf B])$,
the inverse of the group commutator in
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}(4), and
$[\mathsf A,\mathsf B]=-\frac34t_1t_2X_{1,1}$.
The coefficient $\frac14$ is forced instead by GKT's chamber conditions,
which fix the coefficients of $W^{\min}$ and $W^{\max}$, the mixed ones
included.  The mixed factor of the GKT transition must remove the term
$2t_1t_2xy^5$ that its singleton factors leave in the image of $W^{\min}$,
since $W^{\max}$ has no such term; as $X_{1,1}(x^4+y^4)=8x^5y-8xy^5$, this
factor is $\exp(\frac14t_1t_2X_{1,1})$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:first-decoration}).
In part~(2), $p_J!\,q_J!$ is the coefficient of $t_J$ in the image of
$t_1^{p_J}t_2^{q_J}$ under the base change, because $t_i^2=0$ for every
label~$i$.  On the GKT side, the critical generators of a support act on
its balanced monomials through the weighted incidence matrix of a path
graph, which has full column rank and whose one left-kernel relation is the
chamber condition of that support; hence the two extremal chamber indices
determine the logarithms of the factors of the GKT transition uniquely,
support by support
(Proposition~\ref{thm:all-order-decoration}).

Part~(3) is a statement about GKT theory itself and does not involve the
annulus.  GKT realize a transition between two chamber indices by a
homotopy of canonical multisections, with time parameter in $[0,1]$, which
on the critical graphs of a set $J$ of labels vanishes only at one time
$\mathfrak t(J)$, at isolated points; at that time the potential changes by
the exponential of the critical generators whose coefficients are products
of one variable of each label in $J$, weighted, with signs, by these zero
counts (Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}).  Part~(3) belongs to the
comparison because every factor of the GKT transition compared in
parts~(1) and~(2) is the total change of the potential across the time
interval of its ray, for one such compatible homotopy restricted to a finite
system (Section~\ref{sec:gkt-decoration}), and it answers the compatibility
question left open in \cite{wuebben2026boundary}.  The finite systems of
Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system} consist of finitely many labels,
deformation monomials and graph data, and are closed under the following
rule of GKT: along each boundary stratum of the moduli space of a graph, the
multisection is assembled from the multisections on the connected
components of its base, essentially the pieces into which the disks of that
stratum break at their boundary nodes (Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}),
so a system that contains a graph datum contains the data of these
components.  The proof in
Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion} extends canonical multisections and
homotopies relative to the data already chosen, and three features of the
construction make these extensions compatible.  First, with every
deformation monomial that carries a balanced graph, a system contains all
balanced and critical graphs of that monomial; since the chamber conditions
couple the invariants of all balanced graphs of one monomial, and these
invariants are adjusted through the critical graphs of the same monomial,
prescribing the invariants of a new monomial then never alters an old
multisection.
Second, the times assigned to new sets of labels are chosen distinct from
the old times, and away from the times, a set of measure zero, at which an
old homotopy on a graph whose descendent Witten bundle has rank equal to the
dimension and which has at most one boundary marking of the third kind,
restricted to that time, fails to be transverse to zero in the interior of
its moduli space; hence no old homotopy has to be changed.  Third,
the countably many ray factors receive pairwise disjoint time intervals,
ordered by slope, through one embedding of their slope order into the
ordered set of complementary open intervals of a Cantor set, fixed for all
systems.  Each system then restricts to the previous one, and the homotopy
of part~(3) is the inverse limit of these restrictions.

Along one path,
transport through $\mathfrak D$ and transport by the factors of the GKT
transition differ by one
automorphism of the algebra of functions in the chart at the end of the
path, the residual transport of the path; it relates products of functions
transported along that path but not along different paths.  Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence} gives a
degree-weighted correction, a first step towards multiplying functions
carried along different paths, coherent up to an explicit associator, which
is nontrivial for residual transports in general position
(Proposition~\ref{thm:residual-coherence}).

The difference in Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}(1) is invisible to
periods.  For two labels of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ and descendent
degree one, for the potentials of
Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents} with the singleton terms of
$W^{\max}$ and mixed terms $t_1t_2(a\,xy^5+b\,x^5y)$, the mixed period class is
$(6-\frac{a+b}2)[xy\,dx\wedge dy]$.  The GKT chamber condition, which is a
period condition, therefore fixes $a+b=12$, and the flows of the GKT
generator $X_{1,1}$ move $(a,b)$ along the line $a+b=12$ without changing any
period (Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-exact}).  Within that orbit $W^{\max}$
is singled out by a normal form: on each balanced string only the coefficient with
the greatest first exponent survives.  This \emph{maximal normal form} carries
the transport of $W^{\min}$ by $\mathfrak D$ to $W^{\max}$ for every finite
set of such labels (Proposition~\ref{rem:normal-form-general}).  The same happens for
two labels of the same twist, where the GKT transition has a factor on a ray
carrying no wall of $\mathfrak D$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:same-twist}).

\begin{mainproblem}
Construct, from the annular structure of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}, an operation at a boundary circle of
the annulus that realizes the maximal normal form, and so carries the
transport of $W^{\min}$ to the transport of $W^{\max}$
(Problem~\ref{prob:endpoint}).
\end{mainproblem}

\subsection{Further results}

\emph{Two labels of twist $(2,2)$.}  The normalization of the GKT primary
potential in Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration} gives, for two labels of
twist $(2,2)$ and descendent degree zero, the open invariants $-\frac18$ at
the balanced graphs with $(k_1,k_2)=(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$, the \emph{balanced
profiles} of these labels.  These are the invariants of every chamber index
that is \emph{symmetric} in the sense of GKT, that is, invariant under the
permutations of the labels that preserve their twists, and whose primary
potential is the unique one invariant under the exchange of $x$ and $y$, a
symmetry of a different kind (Lemma~\ref{lem:reflection-fixed} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-calibration}).  The sum $-\frac14$ of the two
invariants agrees with
the period computation of \cite[Rem.~4.3]{wuebben2026boundary}; it is the
sum considered in \cite[Ex.~5.5]{grossetal2026survey}, and it differs from
the value $-\frac18$ printed there.

\emph{Three labels.}  The discrepancy of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}(1) recurs for three labels, where
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}(2) gives the wall side.  Let $J$ consist
of three labels of descendent degree one, two of twist $(1,0)$ and one of
twist $(0,1)$, or conversely.  The only critical exponent $k$ of $J$ is
$(2,1)$, respectively $(1,2)$.  The $t_J$-component of the logarithms of the
wall factors is $-\frac34t_JX_k$, which is $S_J$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}(2) with $a_{2,1}=a_{1,2}=-\frac38$,
whereas that of the factors of the GKT transition is $-\frac12t_JX_k$; the
two differ by
$-\frac14t_JX_k$ (Section~\ref{subsec:raywise-factorization}).

\emph{Obstructions.}  Given the dictionary $F$, the annulus is forced, and
the simpler candidates fail.  Exactly one of the three affine monodromies of
the CPS complex preserves $\widetilde M$ (by \eqref{eq:unbranched-conjugates}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-cover}), so $\widetilde M$ is not preserved by
the affine monodromy of the CPS complex and is not the character lattice of
its integral-affine structure.
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}(2), (3) and~(5) record the other
obstructions: for each value of the slab parameter $a_\rho$ no finite Kummer
extension containing the initial roots is stable under transport
(Theorems~\ref{thm:infinite-root-orbit} and~\ref{thm:forced-category}); the
Lie algebra of Hamiltonian derivations of the structure sheaf containing the
two singleton fields is not preserved by the lifted slab maps
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-scalar-frame}); and the local system
$\widetilde M$ on $\Acal^\circ$ has no nonzero global section, even after
pullback to a connected finite cover, so that exponents in one fixed copy of
$\widetilde M$ cannot serve as labels compatible with parallel transport
(Theorem~\ref{thm:theta-local-system} and the discussion after it).  Being an annulus, the base
is not homeomorphic to the intersection complex of a distinguished toric
degeneration of del Pezzo surfaces in the sense of Carl--Pumperla--Siebert,
which is a disk (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-CPS-degeneration}).

\emph{Boundary.}  The walls of $\mathfrak D$ other than the two diagonal
segments leave the annulus transversely through its boundary circles.  Outward
integral-affine cylinders, of widths $4$ and $16$ (the integers $w$ in the
normal forms $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-w\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}(1)), continue the annulus beyond
its boundary circles and separate the exiting walls
(Propositions~\ref{prop:outward-flares} and~\ref{prop:exit-separation}).  A
logarithmic compactification is not constructed for the finite levels, the
finite root stacks of finite subtrees of the path tree, base-changed to a
truncated coefficient ring (Section~\ref{subsec:kummer-charts}): charts
identified along dense opens would force
the holonomy around a boundary circle, a shear, to fix every character, and
the coefficient rings contain no degeneration parameter to absorb it
(Remark~\ref{rem:closing-ends}).  These root stacks and
their Kummer covers are singular in general, and are dominated by log smooth
Deligne--Mumford stacks with simple normal crossings boundary
(Appendix~\ref{app:regular-presentation}).

\emph{Primary Frobenius structure.}  Transport through the annular charts
preserves the primary Frobenius structure of the GKT open mirror theorem
\cite[Thm.~0.3, Cor.~4.17, Cor.~5.14]{grossetal2022open}: the transported
derivatives of the primary potential form a basis of the transported Jacobian
algebra, with product, unit and residue pairing independent of the path
(Proposition~\ref{thm:primary-Frobenius}).  This is transport of structure.
What is specific to the quartic is the normalization of the primary
potential in the chart where the transport starts, which
Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration} determines: the primary potential has
a unique representative invariant under exchanging $x$ and $y$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:reflection-fixed}), and on the marginal line it is the flat
structure of Noumi and Yamada \cite{noumiyamada1998flat}, with primitive form
normalized to $dx\wedge dy$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-calibration}).  The
open invariants for two labels of twist $(2,2)$ stated at the beginning
of this subsection are read off from this normalization.

\subsection{Method and organization}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} has three steps.  The
discrete Legendre transform and the exponents of the two singleton fields
determine the enlarged lattice, the parity cover, and the pathwise Kummer
rings (Section~\ref{sec:affine-kummer}).  A divergence computation shows that the
lifted slab maps do not preserve the Hamiltonian derivations of
$dx\wedge dy$, which leads to the Jacobi bracket on the line bundle
$\Lcal_\kappa$, and
the ordered factorization of the commutator of
the two singleton factors, placed along cofactor directions, gives the
wall structure $\mathfrak D$ (Section~\ref{sec:jacobi-theta}).  The boundary circles
are treated in Section~\ref{sec:compactification}.  For
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}, the GKT notions used are recalled in
Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}; the $\kappa$-shift embeds the GKT critical Lie
algebra, and comparing the increasing-slope factorizations ray by ray gives
the residual factors, the quotients on each ray of the wall factor by the
factor of the GKT transition (Section~\ref{sec:gkt-decoration}).  The compatible
homotopies are built by relative extension of canonical multisections, one
graph and descendent vector at a time in a well-founded order, along the
finite systems of Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}, using
the multisection extension argument of \cite{buryaketal2024} in its $W$-spin
form \cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open} (Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}).
Section~\ref{sec:primary-frobenius} transports the primary Frobenius
structure, and Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents} compares transport through
$\mathfrak D$ with the GKT transition along a path for two labels of
descendent degree one, and states the maximal normalization problem.
Appendix~\ref{app:calculations} records the affine and logarithmic
calculations, Appendix~\ref{app:regular-presentation} the regular
presentation, Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence} products of functions transported
along different paths, and Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration} the normalization
of the GKT primary potential of $x^4+y^4$.  The proofs of
Theorems~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} and~\ref{thm:intro-comparison} do
not use Section~\ref{sec:primary-frobenius} or
Appendices~\ref{app:regular-presentation}, \ref{app:residual-coherence}
and~\ref{app:primary-calibration}, and of Section~\ref{sec:compactification}
they use only the labelled truncations of
Definition~\ref{def:coefficient-window} and the base change
\eqref{eq:window-map}.

The construction follows the separation between affine and wall data of
\cite{grosshackingkeel2015,grossetal2018canonical,grosssiebert2022} but
produces no toric degeneration of the quartic pair, and it does not construct
open FJRW invariants from the annular data.  Its enumerative inputs are the
two singleton Hamiltonian vector fields and, for Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison},
Proposition~\ref{thm:primary-Frobenius} and
Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents}, the GKT critical Lie algebra, the GKT
homotopies, the GKT primary mirror theorem, and the extremal GKT potentials
of the truncations considered.

\emph{Conventions.}  Throughout, $\Bbbk$ is an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero.  Formal-algebraic constructions are made over $\Bbbk$;
oscillatory integrals and Saito theory \cite{saito1983period} are understood
after base change to $\CC$.  The enumerative coefficient rings are defined
over $\QQ$ and are base-changed to $\Bbbk$ when used in the root-stack
construction.
