\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{The question}

The Gross--Siebert program constructs mirrors by placing wall-crossing
automorphisms on an integral-affine base with singularities and reading off
broken lines and theta functions
\cite{grosshackingkeel2015,grossetal2018canonical,grosssiebert2022}.  Its
input is geometric: a base, its singular locus, a polyhedral decomposition,
and a polarization.  This paper asks whether the wall-crossing of an open
Landau--Ginzburg theory supplies such a base, and answers the question for
one model.

Gross, Kelly, and Tessler (GKT) define genus-zero open FJRW invariants for
$(W,G)=(x^r+y^s,\mu_r\times\mu_s)$ as relative Euler numbers on moduli spaces
of $W$-spin disks \cite{grossetal2022open}.  The invariants depend on a choice
of canonical boundary conditions, and changes of boundary conditions act
through a formal wall-crossing group closely related to the tropical vertex
group.  This action has the algebra of a scattering diagram but no
integral-affine realization: no affine manifold, singular locus, or
polyhedral decomposition carries its walls.  Gross, Kelly, and Tessler list a
tropical version of open FJRW theory among the open problems of their survey
\cite[\S6]{grossetal2026survey}, and Maher observes that it is not obvious
how to incorporate Landau--Ginzburg mirror symmetry into the Gross--Siebert
program, since a Landau--Ginzburg model has no target space and hence no SYZ
fibration \cite[Rem.~5.1.1]{maher2024predictions}.

The companion paper \cite{wuebben2026boundary} orders the GKT wall-crossing.
Each generator of the GKT Lie algebra is attached to a critical $W$-spin
graph, and its \emph{boundary ray} is the ray spanned by the pair of numbers
of boundary markings of the two kinds on that graph; this is a ray in the
plane of marking counts, not in any affine base.  Modulo any finite set of
deformation monomials closed under taking divisors, the transition between
the two \emph{extremal} GKT boundary conditions factors uniquely into factors
ordered by the slope of their boundary rays
\cite[Thms.~1.2 and~1.3]{wuebben2026boundary}.  The boundary conditions correspond to GKT's chamber indices
(Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}).  For a nonempty set of labels, the
coefficients of the potential at the balanced graphs of that set form its
\emph{balanced string}, consecutive lattice points on a line.  The extremal
boundary conditions are those in which, on every balanced string, only the
coefficient with the least, respectively greatest, first exponent
survives; we call their potentials the \emph{extremal GKT potentials}
$W^{\min}$ and $W^{\max}$.  The factorization supplies an ordered family of
wall-crossing operations, but no surface on which their rays lie and no
affine monodromy.

We construct such a surface, and a wall structure on it that is consistent at
its joints, for the
parabolic quartic $W=x^4+y^4$, the singularity $X_9$.  Its central charge
$\widehat c=2-\frac2r-\frac2s$ equals one; by \cite{wuebben2026threshold} the
primary invariants are independent of the boundary conditions exactly when
$\widehat c<1$.  The suspension of the marginal pencil of $X_9$ is an
anticanonical elliptic curve in $\PP(1,1,2)$, and the toric-boundary
degeneration of that pair gives the disk-shaped intersection complex of the
Landau--Ginzburg construction of Carl--Pumperla--Siebert \cite{carletal2024},
the \emph{CPS complex}.  It is the natural candidate base.

\subsection{Notation}\label{subsec:intro-notation}

Appendix~\ref{app:notation} lists the principal symbols of the paper.
Let $M\simeq\ZZ^2$ be the lattice of the fan of $\PP(1,1,2)$ (the lattice
denoted $N$ in toric geometry).  We write $M$ for it because it carries the
exponents of all Laurent monomials, slab functions and wall functions below,
as $M$ does in \cite{grosspandharipandesiebert2010}.  It is the character lattice of the dual
torus, which carries the Landau--Ginzburg monomials and the slab functions of
the CPS complex, while the polygon of the CPS complex lies in
$M^\vee\otimes\RR$, with $M^\vee=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\ZZ)$
(Section~\ref{sec:affine-kummer}).  Let $\ZZ^2_{x,y}$ be the lattice of
bidegrees $(a,b)$ of monomials $x^ay^b$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-F}
 F=\frac14\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\0&-2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \widetilde M=F(\ZZ^2_{x,y}),
 \qquad \widetilde M/M\simeq\ZZ/2\oplus\ZZ/4.
\end{equation}
The \emph{exponent map} $F$ sends $x^ay^b$ to the character $z^{F(a,b)}$.
With $\iota=4F$, the \emph{cofactor}
$L=\det(\iota)\,\iota^{-T}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-2&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$
gives the direction $Lq\in M^\vee\otimes\RR$ along which the wall with
shifted exponent $q$ is placed.  In $F$-coordinates, the coordinates in the
basis $F(1,0),F(0,1)$, put $\kappa=(1,1)$, the exponent of $xy$, and
\[
 \Omega_\kappa=z^\kappa\,d\log z_1\wedge d\log z_2=dx\wedge dy,
 \qquad X^\kappa_q=z^{q-\kappa}\partial_{q^\perp},\quad q^\perp=(q_2,-q_1),
\]
where $\partial_v$ is the invariant logarithmic derivation in direction $v$;
thus $X^\kappa_q$ is the Hamiltonian vector field of $x^{q_1}y^{q_2}$ for
$dx\wedge dy$.  We write $X_{a,b}=X^\kappa_{\kappa+(a,b)}$ and
$E_{a,b}=t_1^at_2^bX_{a,b}$, so that $\mathsf A=-\frac12E_{1,0}$ and
$\mathsf B=-\frac12E_{0,1}$.  The \emph{singleton parameters} $t_1,t_2$ are
the variables of a single label, in GKT's terms an internal marking of twist
$(1,0)$, respectively $(0,1)$, carrying one descendent, and
$\mathfrak m=(t_1,t_2)$ is the \emph{singleton ideal}.

The slab function of the slab of length two is
$1+a_\rho z^u+z^{2u}$, with free coefficient $a_\rho$, the \emph{slab
parameter}; the values $a_\rho=\pm2$, at which it is a square, are the
\emph{cusps} (Section~\ref{sec:affine-kummer}).  The annulus $\overline\Acal$
has interior $\Acal$, and $\Acal^\circ$ is the complement of its four
singular points.  The transition at each singular
point is carried by an arc from that point to the boundary; we call these arcs
the \emph{cuts}.  The points $O_0,O_1$ are the two lifts of the central vertex
of the CPS complex (Section~\ref{subsec:kummer-charts}), and the two lines
through $O_0$ carrying $\mathsf A$ and $\mathsf B$ are the \emph{chords}.  The
holonomy along the core loop
$\gamma_{\mathrm{core}}$ of Section~\ref{subsec:parity-cover}, in the basis
$F(1,0),F(0,1)$, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-K}
 K=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\-10&19\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}K=18.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Main results}

The generators of the GKT critical algebra of $x^4+y^4$ attached to a
single internal marking of twist $(1,0)$, respectively $(0,1)$, with one
descendent are, in the notation of Section~\ref{subsec:intro-notation},
\[
 \mathsf A=-\tfrac12t_1X^\kappa_{(2,1)},
 \qquad
 \mathsf B=-\tfrac12t_2X^\kappa_{(1,2)},
\]
the logarithms of the two singleton factors of the GKT transition
\eqref{eq:mixed-GKT-transition}.  Their exponents, with standard coordinates
$F(2,1)=(\frac14,-\frac12)$ and $F(1,2)=(-\frac14,-1)$ in $M\otimes\QQ$,
are fractional: they do not lie in $M$, and together with $M$ they span the
overlattice $\widetilde M$ of index eight.

\begin{maintheorem}[Annular base and wall structure]
\label{thm:intro-reconstruction}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The smallest cover of the CPS complex on which $\widetilde M$ is
preserved is the double cover branched at two of its three singular points.
It is a polarized integral-affine annulus $\overline\Acal$ with integral
tangent lattice $\widetilde M^\vee$, so that exponents lie in the dual
lattice $\widetilde M$, with two focus--focus points $B_0,B_1$, and two
ramification points $\widetilde A,\widetilde C$ of the cover at which the
structure is the pullback of a focus--focus germ under $z\mapsto z^2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-cover}).  Its holonomy along a core loop has trace
$18$, and its monodromies around the two boundary circles are conjugate to
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-4\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-16\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
\item Lifting the slab transitions requires roots of the slab functions, of
degrees $2,4,4,2$ at $\widetilde A,B_0,B_1,\widetilde C$ (the degree at
$B_0,B_1$ drops to two at the cusps).  Their orbit under transport is
infinite, so no finite root stack contains it, while the functions
transported along any finite set of paths lie in a single finite Kummer
extension (Theorems~\ref{thm:infinite-root-orbit}
and~\ref{thm:forced-category}).
\item The lifted slab maps rescale the volume form $dx\wedge dy$ by roots of
the slab functions.  The Hamiltonian bracket therefore glues only as a
Jacobi bracket on a line bundle $\Lcal_\kappa$ over the Kummer
covers (Propositions~\ref{prop:no-scalar-frame}
and~\ref{prop:Jacobi-transport}).
\item Place $\mathsf A$ and $\mathsf B$ on the two lines through a lift $O_0$
of the central vertex in the directions $L(2,1)$ and $L(1,2)$.  On the ray
from $O_0$ in direction $Lq$, place the inverse of the factor on the ray of
$q$ in the ordered factorization of the group commutator
$e^{\mathsf A}e^{-\mathsf B}e^{-\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}$, and transport the
result to the other lift $O_1$.  This gives a proper, locally finite wall
structure $\mathfrak D$ on the complement $\Acal^\circ$ of the singular
points, consistent at $O_0$ and $O_1$ at every finite order
(Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency}).  Consistency at the joints depends
on the placement only through the cyclic order of the supports about $O_0$,
which is that of the exponents and, on the chords, their negatives.
\item The core holonomy acts hyperbolically on $\widetilde M$, with no
nonzero finite orbit, so exponents in one fixed copy of $\widetilde M$ cannot
label a basis of sections compatibly with transport
(Theorem~\ref{thm:theta-local-system}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{maintheorem}

The walls of $\mathfrak D$ are not tangent to the exponents of their
functions, as they are in
\cite{grosspandharipandesiebert2010,grossetal2016theta}: the exponents lie
on the Legendre-dual side, and the supports in the plane of the polygon.
Tangency transported by the discrete Legendre transform would place each wall
along the annihilator of its exponent, a rule incompatible with the exchange
of $x$ and $y$; the compatible linear rules are the gradient maps of
exchange-invariant quadratic functions, and the cofactor is one of them
(Section~\ref{subsec:exponent-lattices}).  Consistency is the local
condition around the two joints; nothing is imposed around a singular point
or around the core, and the diagonal walls end at $B_0$ and $B_1$.  Beyond the
Kontsevich--Soibelman factorization at a point, the content of $\mathfrak D$
lies in the annular cover that carries it, the cyclic order of the cofactor
directions, and the fact that the walls avoid the cuts
(Proposition~\ref{prop:proper-supports}).

The $\kappa$-shift identifies each GKT generator $X_k$ with the Hamiltonian
field $X^\kappa_{k+\kappa}$ of $x^{k_1+1}y^{k_2+1}$ for $dx\wedge dy$, so the
GKT critical Lie algebra of every finite set of labels embeds, without change
of coefficients, in the Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_\kappa$ of Hamiltonian
derivations for the Jacobi bracket (Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-embedding} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-operations}).  The comparison of $\mathfrak D$
with GKT wall-crossing is then as follows.

\begin{maintheorem}[Comparison with GKT wall-crossing]
\label{thm:intro-comparison}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The wall structure $\mathfrak D$ is not the GKT wall-crossing.  For two labels
of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ with one descendent each, the first ray
carrying both descendent parameters has coefficient $-3/4$ in $\mathfrak D$
and $1/4$ in the GKT transition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:first-decoration}), and the path-ordered product of
$\mathfrak D$ along a path passing the joint carries $W^{\min}$ to
$\exp(-t_1t_2X_{1,1})\,W^{\max}$ rather than to $W^{\max}$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport}).
\item Fix a finite set of labels of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ with one
descendent each, and let $J$ be a subset consisting of $p_J$ labels of twist
$(1,0)$ and $q_J$ of twist $(0,1)$.  After the base change sending $t_1$
and $t_2$ to the sums of the variables $t_i$ of the labels of twist $(1,0)$
and $(0,1)$, respectively, the $t_J$-component of the logarithms of the wall factors of $\mathfrak D$ is
\[
 S_J=p_J!\,q_J!\,a_{p_J,q_J}\,t_JX_{p_J,q_J},
\]
where $a_{p,q}$ is the coefficient of $E_{p,q}$ in the ordered factorization
of $e^{\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}$ ($a_{p,q}=-c_{p,q}$ for $p,q\geq1$, with
$c_{p,q}$ in Table~\ref{tab:singleton-coefficients}).  It agrees with the
$t_J$-component of the logarithms of the GKT factors when $|J|=1$, and not
when $J$ consists of one label of each twist, where it is
$-\frac34t_JX_{1,1}$ and the GKT component is $\frac14t_JX_{1,1}$
(Proposition~\ref{thm:all-order-decoration}).
\item The finite homotopies of canonical multisections that realize the GKT
transitions can be chosen compatibly as the set of labels grows
(Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization} and
Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{maintheorem}

Part~(3) belongs to the comparison because every GKT factor compared in
parts~(1) and~(2) is the jump, at its assigned time, of a finite restriction
of one such compatible homotopy (Section~\ref{sec:gkt-decoration}).  It
answers the compatibility question left open in
\cite{wuebben2026boundary}; the systems of labels and monomials for which it
holds are those of Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}.  Along one path,
transport through $\mathfrak D$ and transport by GKT's factors differ by one
automorphism of the target chart algebra, the residual transport of the
path; it relates products of functions transported along that path but not
along different paths.  Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence} gives a
degree-weighted correction, a first step towards multiplying functions
carried along different paths, coherent up to an explicit associator that is
nontrivial in general.

The difference in Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}(1) is invisible to
periods.  For two labels of twists $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ with one descendent
each, for the potentials of
Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents} with the singleton terms of
$W^{\max}$ and mixed terms $t_1t_2(a\,xy^5+b\,x^5y)$, the mixed period class is
$(6-\frac{a+b}2)[xy\,dx\wedge dy]$.  The GKT chamber condition, which is a
period condition, therefore fixes $a+b=12$, and the flows of the GKT
generator $X_{1,1}$ move $(a,b)$ along the line $a+b=12$ without changing any
period (Proposition~\ref{prop:relative-exact}).  Within that orbit $W^{\max}$
is singled out by a normal form: on each balanced string only the coefficient with
the greatest first exponent survives.  This \emph{maximal normal form} carries
the transport of $W^{\min}$ by $\mathfrak D$ to $W^{\max}$ for every finite
set of such labels (Proposition~\ref{rem:normal-form-general}).  The same happens for
two labels of the same twist, where the GKT transition has a factor on a ray
carrying no wall of $\mathfrak D$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:same-twist}).

\begin{mainproblem}
Construct, from the annular structure of
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}, an operation at a boundary circle of
the annulus that realizes the maximal normal form, and so carries the
transport of $W^{\min}$ to $W^{\max}$ (Problem~\ref{prob:endpoint}).
\end{mainproblem}

\subsection{Further results}

\emph{Obstructions.}  Given the dictionary $F$, the annulus is forced, and
the simpler candidates fail.  Exactly one of the three affine monodromies of
the CPS complex preserves $\widetilde M$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:integral-cofactor},
\eqref{eq:unbranched-conjugates} and Theorem~\ref{thm:parity-cover}), so
$\widetilde M$ is not the character lattice of any integral-affine structure
on the CPS complex or on a root stack whose coarse space is the CPS complex.
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction}(2), (3) and~(5) record the other
obstructions: for each value of the slab parameter $a_\rho$ no finite Kummer
extension containing the initial roots is stable under transport
(Theorems~\ref{thm:infinite-root-orbit} and~\ref{thm:forced-category}); the
Lie algebra of Hamiltonian derivations of the structure sheaf containing the
two singleton fields is not preserved by the lifted slab maps
(Proposition~\ref{prop:no-scalar-frame}); and exponents in one fixed copy of
$\widetilde M$ cannot label a global basis of sections compatibly with
transport (Theorem~\ref{thm:theta-local-system}).  Being an annulus, the base
is not homeomorphic to the intersection complex of a distinguished toric
degeneration of del Pezzo surfaces in the sense of Carl--Pumperla--Siebert,
which is a disk (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-CPS-degeneration}).

\emph{Boundary.}  The walls of $\mathfrak D$ other than the two diagonal
segments leave the annulus transversely through its boundary circles.  Outward
integral-affine cylinders of widths $4$ and $16$ continue the collars and
separate the exiting walls (Propositions~\ref{prop:outward-flares}
and~\ref{prop:exit-separation}).  A logarithmic compactification of the finite
stages is not constructed: charts identified along dense opens would force
the holonomy around a boundary circle, a shear, to fix every character, and
the coefficient rings contain no degeneration parameter to absorb it
(Remark~\ref{rem:closing-ends}).  The finite root-stack presentations and
their Kummer covers are singular in general, and are dominated by log smooth
Deligne--Mumford stacks with simple normal crossings boundary
(Appendix~\ref{app:regular-presentation}).

\emph{Primary Frobenius structure.}  Transport through the annular charts
preserves the primary Frobenius structure of the GKT open mirror theorem
\cite[Thm.~0.3, Cor.~4.17, Cor.~5.14]{grossetal2022open}: the transported
derivatives of the primary potential form a basis of the transported Jacobian
algebra, with product, unit and residue pairing independent of the path
(Proposition~\ref{thm:primary-Frobenius}).  This is transport of structure.
What is specific to the quartic is the source normalization, which
Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration} calibrates: the primary potential has a unique representative invariant under exchanging $x$ and $y$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:reflection-fixed}), and on the marginal line it is the flat
structure of Noumi and Yamada \cite{noumiyamada1998flat}, with primitive form
normalized to $dx\wedge dy$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-calibration}).

\subsection{Method and organization}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-reconstruction} has three steps.  The
discrete Legendre transform and the descendent exponents determine the
enlarged lattice, the parity cover, and the pathwise Kummer rings
(Section~\ref{sec:affine-kummer}).  A divergence computation shows that the
lifted slab maps are not Poisson for $\Omega_\kappa$, which forces the
Jacobi bracket, and the ordered factorization of the commutator of
the two incoming factors, placed along cofactor directions, gives the
diagram $\mathfrak D$ (Section~\ref{sec:jacobi-theta}).  The boundary circles
are treated in Section~\ref{sec:compactification}.  For
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}, the GKT notions used are recalled in
Section~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}; the $\kappa$-shift embeds the GKT Lie
algebra, and comparing the increasing-slope factorizations ray by ray gives
the residual factors (Section~\ref{sec:gkt-decoration}).  The compatible
homotopies are built by relative extension of canonical multisections, datum
by datum in a well-founded order on finite assembling-closed systems, using
the multisection extension argument of \cite{buryaketal2024} in its $W$-spin
form \cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open} (Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}).
Section~\ref{sec:primary-frobenius} transports the primary Frobenius
structure, and Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents} computes the first
descendent truncations and states the maximal normalization problem.
Appendix~\ref{app:calculations} records the affine and logarithmic
calculations, Appendix~\ref{app:regular-presentation} the regular
presentation, Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence} products of functions transported
along different paths, and Appendix~\ref{app:primary-calibration} the calibration
of the GKT primary potential of $x^4+y^4$.

The construction follows the separation between affine and wall data of
\cite{grosshackingkeel2015,grossetal2018canonical,grosssiebert2022} but
produces no toric degeneration of the quartic pair, and it does not construct
open FJRW invariants from the annular data.  Its enumerative inputs are the
two singleton Hamiltonians and, for Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison},
Proposition~\ref{thm:primary-Frobenius} and
Section~\ref{sec:positive-descendents}, the GKT critical algebra, the GKT
homotopies, the GKT primary mirror theorem, and the extremal GKT potentials
of the truncations considered.

\emph{Conventions.}  Throughout, $\Bbbk$ is an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero.  Formal-algebraic constructions are made over $\Bbbk$;
oscillatory integrals and Saito theory \cite{saito1983period} are understood
after base change to $\CC$.  The enumerative coefficient rings are defined
over $\QQ$ and are base-changed to $\Bbbk$ when used in the root-stack
construction.
