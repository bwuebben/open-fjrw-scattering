\section{Comparison with Gross--Kelly--Tessler wall-crossing}
\label{sec:gkt-decoration}

The diagram $\mathfrak D$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency} is
determined by the two singleton factors and by consistency at its joints.
The wall-crossing group of Gross, Kelly and Tessler has a different origin:
its elements are the transitions between the systems of open invariants
defined by different canonical multisections.  After the $\kappa$-shift
both act through the Hamiltonian wall algebra $\mathfrak g_\kappa$ of
Section~\ref{sec:jacobi-theta}, the Lie algebra of $\Omega_\kappa$-Hamiltonian
derivations spanned by the fields $X^\kappa_q$ of
\eqref{eq:shifted-Hamiltonian} with the bracket \eqref{eq:shifted-bracket},
but their coefficients need not agree.  We compare them one ray at a time.

\subsection{Recollections from Gross--Kelly--Tessler}
\label{subsec:gkt-recollections}

We recall the notions of \cite{grossetal2022open} used in this section and
in Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}, for $W=x^4+y^4$; thus $r=s=4$ in the
notation of \cite{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{Labels and coefficients.}  Internal marked points carry labels from a
fixed countable set.  Each label $i$ has a twist
$\tau(i)=(a_i,b_i)\in\{0,1,2,3\}^2$, and every twist is carried by
infinitely many labels \cite[\S0.1]{grossetal2022open}.  All constructions
are bounded by a finite set $I$ of labels.  A descendent vector on
$J\subseteq I$ is $\mathbf d=(d_i)_{i\in J}\in\ZZ_{\geq0}^J$, and for
$J\neq\varnothing$ we write $r_J,s_J\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ for the residues of
$\sum_{i\in J}a_i$ and $\sum_{i\in J}b_i$ modulo $4$.  The coefficient ring is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:labelled-ring}
 A_I
 =\QQ[\{u_{i,d}:i\in I, d\geq0\}]
  /\langle u_{i,d}u_{i,d'}:i\in I\rangle
\end{equation}
\cite[Def.~4.6]{grossetal2022open}, in which two variables belonging to the
same label have zero product, and $u_{J,\mathbf d}=\prod_{i\in J}u_{i,d_i}$.

\emph{Graphs and bundles.}  The graphs are the graded open $(4,4)$-graphs
$\Gamma$, the dual graphs of genus-zero graded $W$-spin disks, with internal
labels $I(\Gamma)\subseteq I$ and unlabelled boundary tails
\cite[Defs.~1.41 and~3.21]{grossetal2022open}.  A smooth connected graph has
one vertex and, besides its internal tails, $k_1(\Gamma)$ boundary
\emph{$r$-tails}, of twist $(2,0)$, $k_2(\Gamma)$ boundary \emph{$s$-tails},
of twist $(0,2)$, and $k_{12}(\Gamma)$ \emph{fully twisted} boundary tails,
of twist $(2,2)$ \cite[\S2.4 and Notation~2.27]{grossetal2022open}; the names
of the first two kinds refer to the exponents of $x^r+y^s$.  The moduli
space $\overline{\mathcal M}{}^W_\Gamma$ of such disks carries the descendent
Witten bundle $E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$, the direct sum of the Witten bundle and,
for each $i\in I(\Gamma)$, of $d_i$ copies of the cotangent line at the
$i$th internal marking \cite[Def.~2.16]{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{Balanced and critical graphs.}  A smooth connected graph with one fully
twisted tail is \emph{balanced} for $\mathbf d$ if its moduli space is nonempty
and
$\operatorname{rank}E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)=\dim\overline{\mathcal M}{}^W_\Gamma$
\cite[Def.~2.32]{grossetal2022open}.  A smooth connected graph without fully
twisted tails that is relevant, that is, not irrelevant in the sense of
\cite[Def.~1.57]{grossetal2022open}, is \emph{critical} for $\mathbf d$ if
the rank exceeds the dimension by one \cite[Def.~2.33(1)]{grossetal2022open}.
Write $\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)$ for the set of balanced graphs
with internal labels $J$.  Such a graph $\Gamma$ is determined by its numbers
$(k_1(\Gamma),k_2(\Gamma))$ of $r$-tails and $s$-tails, and these pairs, for
$\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)$, are the pairs $(k_1,k_2)$ with
$k_1\equiv r_J$, $k_2\equiv s_J$ modulo $4$ and
\[
 k_1+k_2=4+\sum_{i\in J}\bigl(a_i+b_i+4(d_i-1)\bigr)
\]
\cite[Prop.~2.31 and Notation~2.40]{grossetal2022open}.  A critical graph with
$k_1+1$ $r$-tails and $k_2+1$ $s$-tails has
\emph{critical exponent} $(k_1,k_2)$; the critical exponents of
$(J,\mathbf d)$ satisfy the same congruences and
$k_1+k_2=\sum_{i\in J}(a_i+b_i+4(d_i-1))$
\cite[Prop.~2.39 and Notation~2.40]{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{Boundary strata.}  Let $\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ be the complement in
$\overline{\mathcal M}{}^W_\Gamma$ of the strata of boundary graphs with a
positive boundary half-edge, the space written
$\overline{\mathcal{PM}}_\Gamma$ in \cite[Notation~3.1]{grossetal2022open}.  Its
boundary is the union of the strata $\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda$ of two kinds of
boundary graphs $\Lambda$ \cite[Def.~3.2]{grossetal2022open}.  The
\emph{exchange boundary graphs} $\Lambda\in\partial^{\mathrm{exchange}}\Gamma$
have a vertex which, after detaching one boundary edge, carries exactly three
boundary half-edges with the twists of \cite[Def.~1.54]{grossetal2022open};
two such strata that differ by reversing the cyclic order at that vertex carry
matching boundary conditions, and their contributions to zero counts cancel
(\cite{grossetal2022open}, discussion after Def.~1.56, and
\cite[Lem.~5.3]{grossetal2022open}).  The \emph{zero
boundary graphs} $\Lambda\in\partial^0\Gamma$ are the remaining boundary
graphs with a boundary edge or a contracted boundary half-edge
\cite[Def.~1.56]{grossetal2022open}.  For $\Lambda$ of either kind, the
\emph{base} $B\Lambda$ is obtained by detaching the boundary edges at which,
for each grading, one half-edge has twist and alternation zero, and
forgetting the boundary tails of twist and alternation zero; the induced map
is $F_\Lambda:\overline{\mathcal M}{}^W_\Lambda\to\overline{\mathcal
M}{}^W_{B\Lambda}$ \cite[Def.~3.5 and Notation~3.6]{grossetal2022open}, and
the Witten bundle over $\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda$ is pulled back along it
\cite[Obs.~3.11]{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{Canonical multisections.}  A multisection of $E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$ is
\emph{strongly positive} if, near the removed strata and along the strata of
irrelevant graphs, its Witten components evaluate positively, with respect
to the gradings, at the boundary points and on the boundary intervals
specified in \cite[Defs.~3.17 and~3.18]{grossetal2022open}; this keeps zeros
from leaving through these strata.  A \emph{family of canonical multisections bounded by
$I$} is a collection $\mathfrak s=(s_{\Gamma,\mathbf d})$ of strongly positive
multisections $s_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}$ of $E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$ over
$\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$, one for every graph $\Gamma$ with
$I(\Gamma)\subseteq I$ and every descendent vector $\mathbf d$, that satisfy
the assembling relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GKT-assembling}
 s_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}|_{\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda}
 =F_\Lambda^*
 \left(\boxplus_{\Xi\in\operatorname{Conn}(B\Lambda)}
 s_{\Xi,\mathbf d|_{I(\Xi)}}\right)
\end{equation}
for every zero or exchange boundary graph $\Lambda$ of $\Gamma$; here
$\boxplus$ is the external sum over the connected components $\Xi$ of the
base \cite[Defs.~3.20, 3.27 and~3.28]{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{Invariants and chamber indices.}  For a transverse family
$\mathfrak s$ and $\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)$, the
invariant $\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}\in\QQ$ is the weighted count
of zeros of $s_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}$ \cite[(3.20) and
Def.~3.32]{grossetal2022open}.  The vector $\nu^{\mathfrak s}$ lies in
$\QQ\text{-}\mathrm{Bal}(I)=\prod_{J,\mathbf d}
\QQ^{\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)}$ \cite[(0.3)]{grossetal2022open};
for $\nu$ in this space and $\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)$
with $b=(k_1(\Gamma),k_2(\Gamma))$ we write $\nu_{J,b}$ for the coordinate
of $\nu$ at $\Gamma$.  With the quartic Gamma weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic-Gamma-weight}
 w_J(u,v)=
 \frac{\Gamma((u+1)/4)\Gamma((v+1)/4)}
      {\Gamma((r_J+1)/4)\Gamma((s_J+1)/4)},
\end{equation}
which is nonzero for $u,v\geq0$, put
\[
 A(J,\mathbf d,\nu)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Part}(J)}\ \sum_{(b_C)_{C\in\pi}}
 w_J\Bigl(\sum_{C\in\pi}b_C\Bigr)\prod_{C\in\pi}\nu_{C,b_C},
\]
where $\operatorname{Part}(J)$ is the set of set partitions of $J$ and $b_C$
runs over the pairs $(k_1(\Gamma),k_2(\Gamma))$ of the graphs
$\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(C,\mathbf d|_C)$
\cite[Notation~3.42]{grossetal2022open}.  A \emph{chamber index} bounded by
$I$ is an element $\nu$ such that $\nu$ equals $1$ on balanced graphs with one
internal marking and $\mathbf d=0$ and $-1$ on balanced graphs without
internal markings; $A(\{i\},(d),\nu)=(-1)^d$; and $A(J,\mathbf d,\nu)=0$
whenever $|J|\geq2$ and $4+\sum_{i\in J}(a_i+b_i+4(d_i-1))\geq r_J+s_J$
\cite[Def.~3.47]{grossetal2022open}.  The chamber indices are exactly the
vectors $\nu^{\mathfrak s}$ of transverse canonical families
\cite[Cor.~5.14]{grossetal2022open}.  The potential of $\nu$ is
\[
 W^\nu=\sum_{J\subseteq I,\ \mathbf d\in\ZZ^J_{\geq0}}\
 \sum_{\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)}
 (-1)^{|J|-1}\nu_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}\,
 x^{k_1(\Gamma)}y^{k_2(\Gamma)}u_{J,\mathbf d}
 \in A_I[[x,y]]
\]
\cite[Def.~4.6]{grossetal2022open}.

\emph{The wall-crossing group.}  The quartic critical Lie algebra
\cite[Def.~4.23]{grossetal2022open} is spanned over $\QQ$ by the elements
$u_{J,\mathbf d}X_k$ with $\varnothing\neq J\subseteq I$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-conditions}
 k\equiv\sum_{i\in J}\tau(i)\pmod4,
 \qquad
 k_1+k_2=\sum_{i\in J}\bigl(a_i+b_i+4(d_i-1)\bigr),
\end{equation}
that is, with $k$ a critical exponent of $(J,\mathbf d)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GKT-generator}
 X_k=x^{k_1}y^{k_2}
 \bigl((k_2+1)x\partial_x-(k_1+1)y\partial_y\bigr).
\end{equation}
The definition in \cite{grossetal2022open} excludes $k=(0,0)$.  We adjoin
the degree-zero generator $X_{0,0}=x\partial_x-y\partial_y$ whenever
$k=(0,0)$ satisfies \eqref{eq:critical-conditions}.  The exponential group of
the critical Lie algebra acts on $A_I[[x,y]]$ \cite[Def.~4.19 and
(4.18)]{grossetal2022open}, and on chamber indices by $g(W^\nu)=W^{g(\nu)}$, faithfully and transitively
\cite[Thm.~4.27 and Cor.~5.14]{grossetal2022open}; the group enlarged by
$X_{0,0}$ acts freely and transitively \cite[Lem.~2.5]{wuebben2026boundary}.  A \emph{canonical
homotopy} is a multisection over $[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ that is
strongly positive at every time and satisfies the assembling relation in the
form of \cite[Def.~3.36]{grossetal2022open}; it is \emph{simple} if on every
boundary stratum it is assembled from one homotopy for each component of the
base, independent of the stratum.  We write $\vartheta\in[0,1]$ for the time
parameter of a homotopy.  Between two transverse families,
\cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open} provides a family of simple canonical
homotopies and a distinct time $\vartheta_J\in(0,1)$ for every nonempty
$J\subseteq I$ such that, on a critical graph with internal labels $J$, the
homotopy vanishes only at time $\vartheta_J$, at isolated points.  The proof of
\cite[Thm.~5.9]{grossetal2022open} shows that at time $\vartheta_J$ the
potential changes by the exponential of the critical generators with support
$J$, weighted by $(-1)^{|J|+k_2}$ times the zero counts of the homotopy on the
corresponding critical graphs.

\subsection{Labelled critical algebras}

Let $Q=\widetilde M/4\widetilde M\simeq(\ZZ/4)^2$ be the twist group of
\eqref{eq:GKT-residue-group}, the group of GKT twists modulo $4$.  An
object $\mathcal I=(I,\tau)$ consists of a finite set of labels and a twist
map $\tau:I\to Q$; morphisms are twist-preserving bijections.  We write $A_{\mathcal I}$ for the ring
\eqref{eq:labelled-ring} of $I$, and $\tau(i)=(a_i,b_i)$ with representatives
in $\{0,1,2,3\}^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Labelled critical-algebra embedding]
\label{thm:critical-embedding}
For every $\mathcal I$, the assignment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-embedding}
 \jmath_{\mathcal I}:
 u_{J,\mathbf d}X_k
 \longmapsto
 u_{J,\mathbf d}X_{k+\kappa}^{\kappa}
\end{equation}
is a natural coefficient-preserving injective Lie map from the GKT critical
algebra, enlarged by the degree-zero generator, to the Hamiltonian wall
algebra $\mathfrak g_\kappa\otimes A_{\mathcal I}$ in the frame of the sheet
$S_0$.  It sends $X_{0,0}$ to $X_\kappa^\kappa$.  Jacobi transport along
paths in the twist cover, on which the $Q$-grading is constant,
carries this embedding to the frame of every other region of $\mathfrak D$,
compatibly with composition of paths.  The construction commutes with
relabelling, with restriction to a subset of the labels, and with reduction
modulo powers of the ideal generated by the $u_{i,d}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Specializing the bracket formula \cite[(4.17)]{grossetal2022open} to two
variables gives
\[
 [X_k,X_\ell]
 =-\det(k+\kappa,\ell+\kappa)X_{k+\ell}.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:shifted-bracket} gives the same formula after applying
\eqref{eq:critical-embedding}.  If two coefficient monomials share a label,
their product vanishes in \eqref{eq:labelled-ring}; if their supports are
disjoint, both conditions in \eqref{eq:critical-conditions} add, as in
\cite[Prop.~4.25]{grossetal2022open}.  Hence the
assignment is a Lie map, and it is injective on the displayed monomial
basis.  The bracket with the degree-zero generator is preserved because
\[
 [X_{0,0},X_{a,b}]=(a-b)X_{a,b}
 =-\det(\kappa,(a,b)+\kappa)X_{a,b}.
\]
Jacobi path transport is a Lie isomorphism by
Proposition~\ref{prop:Jacobi-transport}, and it is compatible with
composition of paths.  On the twist cover the
$Q$-grading is constant; affine transport moves the Hamiltonian exponent and
the $\kappa$-shift together.  The remaining naturality statements are
immediate from the labelled formula.
\end{proof}

Transport along the twist cover is not induced by a change of the
twists of the labels.  The monodromy about each singular point acts on $Q$ by
the matrix $P_{\mathrm{res}}$ of \eqref{eq:residue-matrix}, which permutes the
singleton twists cyclically,
\[
 (1,0)\mapsto(2,3)\mapsto(3,2)\mapsto(0,1)\mapsto(1,0).
\]
Replacing the twist $\tau(i)$ of every label by $P_{\mathrm{res}}\tau(i)$,
with the labels and descendent degrees unchanged, does not induce an
isomorphism of critical algebras that maps the $u_{J,\mathbf d}$-component to
the $u_{J,\mathbf d}$-component.  Indeed, for two labels of twist $(1,0)$ and
descendent degree one, the conditions \eqref{eq:critical-conditions} have the
single solution $k=(2,0)$, whereas for two labels of twist $(2,3)$ they have
the three solutions $(0,10)$, $(4,6)$ and $(8,2)$.  Jacobi transport instead
moves the exponent and the volume frame together, and in the target chart it
may produce Laurent expressions whose coefficients involve roots of the slab
functions.

Under the dictionary $x^ay^b\leftrightarrow z^{(a,b)}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:jacobi-theta}, the field $X^\kappa_{k+\kappa}$ is the vector
field $X_k$ of \eqref{eq:GKT-generator}; accordingly $X_{a,b}$ denotes both,
as in the notation $X_{a,b}=X^\kappa_{\kappa+(a,b)}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:introduction}.  For a ray $\mathfrak r$, the \emph{ray group}
of $\mathfrak r$ with coefficients in $A_{\mathcal I}$ is the exponential of
the span of the elements $uX^\kappa_q$ with $q\in\mathfrak r$ and $u$ in the
ideal of $A_{\mathcal I}$ generated by the $u_{i,d}$; it is abelian by
\eqref{eq:shifted-bracket}.

In a finite nilpotent quotient, exponential integrates
\eqref{eq:critical-embedding} to an injective group homomorphism.  Let $\nu$
and $\nu'$ be chamber indices bounded by $I$.  The family of simple canonical
homotopies of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open} between transverse
families realizing them changes the potential at the successive times
$\vartheta_J$ by the exponentials recalled in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections} \cite[Thm.~5.9, with
Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}; their product $g$ satisfies
$g(W^\nu)=W^{\nu'}$, and we call $g$ a \emph{GKT transition} from $\nu$ to
$\nu'$.  The slope-ordered factorization of
\cite[Thms.~1.2 and~1.3]{wuebben2026boundary} writes it uniquely as a product
of factors, each supported on the generators $X_k$ whose shifted exponents
$k+\kappa$ lie on one ray, ordered by increasing slope of that ray; we call
the factor on a ray the \emph{GKT factor} on that ray.  The \emph{extremal
chamber indices} $\nu^{\min}$ and $\nu^{\max}$ are the unique chamber indices
bounded by $I$ such that, for every $(J,\mathbf d)$ with $J\neq\varnothing$,
the only coordinate at
the balanced graphs of $(J,\mathbf d)$ that may be nonzero is the one of
least, respectively greatest, $k_1$ \cite[Thm.~1.3]{wuebben2026boundary}.
Their potentials $W^{\min}=W^{\nu^{\min}}$ and $W^{\max}=W^{\nu^{\max}}$ are
the \emph{extremal GKT potentials}.

\begin{proposition}[Finite GKT transitions]
\label{prop:endpoint-operations}
In every finite nilpotent quotient, every GKT transition maps to an
automorphism of the chart algebra of $S_0$ in the group generated by the ray
groups with coefficients in $A_{\mathcal I}$, and its GKT factors map factor
by factor to the ray groups of the shifted rays $\RR_{\geq0}(k+\kappa)$.  The
GKT transition from $\nu$ to $\nu'$, and hence its image, depends only on
$\nu$ and $\nu'$, not on the chosen canonical homotopy.  The construction is
natural under labelled restriction and path transport.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exponentiation and Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff are finite in the chosen
quotient and are functorial for the map of Theorem
\ref{thm:critical-embedding}.  GKT prove that the action of their group on
chamber indices is faithful and transitive
\cite[Thm.~4.27 and Cor.~5.14]{grossetal2022open}; Step~2 of the proof of
\cite[Thm.~4.27]{grossetal2022open} in fact shows that stabilizers are
trivial.  Because a transition may use the degree-zero summand excluded in
\cite[Def.~4.23]{grossetal2022open}, we use the enlarged-torsor statement
\cite[Lem.~2.5]{wuebben2026boundary}: the action of the enlarged group, which
contains the degree-zero generator, is free and transitive.  Its freeness
makes the element carrying $W^\nu$ to $W^{\nu'}$ unique.  The slope
assertion follows because $k$ maps to the shifted exponent $k+\kappa$.
\end{proof}

The finite homotopies realizing GKT transitions can be chosen compatibly as
the labelled system grows (Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization},
proved in Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion}).  Every GKT factor used below is
therefore the jump of a finite homotopy of canonical multisections.

\subsection{The first mixed ray}

Take two distinct labels with
\[
 t_1=u_{i,1},\quad\tau(i)=(1,0),
 \qquad
 t_2=u_{j,1},\quad\tau(j)=(0,1),
\]
and work modulo $(t_1^2,t_2^2)$.  By \cite[Def.~4.6]{grossetal2022open}, the
potential of a chamber index $\nu$ has coefficient
$(-1)^{|J|-1}\nu_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}$ on the monomial of a balanced graph
$\Gamma$ with internal labels $J$.  The extremal GKT potentials are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-potentials}
\begin{aligned}
 W^{\min}
 &=x^4+y^4-4t_1xy^4-2t_2y^5+12t_1t_2xy^5,\\
 W^{\max}
 &=x^4+y^4-2t_1x^5-4t_2x^4y+12t_1t_2x^5y.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The GKT transition from $\nu^{\min}$ to $\nu^{\max}$, written in increasing
slope with the rightmost factor acting first, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-GKT-transition}
 \exp\!\left(-\frac12t_2X_{0,1}\right)
 \exp\!\left(\frac14t_1t_2X_{1,1}\right)
 \exp\!\left(-\frac12t_1X_{1,0}\right).
\end{equation}
Write $\Sigma_1=\exp(-\frac12t_1X_{1,0})=e^{\mathsf A}$ and
$\Sigma_2=\exp(-\frac12t_2X_{0,1})=e^{\mathsf B}$ for its two singleton
factors, and for $c\in\QQ$ put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-total-factor}
 Z_c=\exp\!\left(c\,t_1t_2X_{1,1}\right).
\end{equation}
Each $Z_c$ lies in the ray group of the diagonal ray, is central in the group
generated by $\Sigma_1$, $\Sigma_2$ and the $Z_{c'}$, and $Z_cZ_{c'}=Z_{c+c'}$.

With the coorientations of Section~\ref{subsec:cofactor-supports}, a path
that passes the joint $O_0$ from left to right on the side of the outgoing
walls, such as the
path $\gamma_+$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport}, crosses the
positive half-chords and the outgoing walls in increasing shifted slope and
crosses each of them positively; this is the order in which the GKT
transition applies its factors.  The path $\gamma_-$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport} crosses only the two negative
half-chords, also positively.  Modulo $(t_1^2,t_2^2)$ the factors
$\exp(-C_{\mathfrak r})$ of Section~\ref{subsec:cofactor-supports} are the
identity except on the diagonal ray, so both halves of the two chords carry
$\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$, and the first mixed wall, the diagonal wall from
$O_0$ to $B_0$, carries $Z_{-3/4}$, the image of $\exp(-\frac34E_{1,1})$.
The GKT factor and the residual factor on the first mixed ray are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixed-decoration}
 G_{\mathrm{mix}}=Z_{1/4},
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{mix}}=Z_{-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Factorization on the first mixed ray]
\label{prop:first-decoration}
Modulo $(t_1^2,t_2^2)$, the two chords of $\mathfrak D$ carry the GKT
singleton factors $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$, and the first mixed wall carries
$Z_{-3/4}$.  The elements $Z_{-3/4}$, $G_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and
$R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ lie in the ray group of the first mixed ray, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:three-quarter-split}
 Z_{-3/4}=G_{\mathrm{mix}}R_{\mathrm{mix}},
 \qquad -\frac34=\frac14-1.
\end{equation}
Here $G_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is the mixed factor of the GKT transition
\eqref{eq:mixed-GKT-transition}, which is unique by
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-operations}; hence $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is
determined by the wall.  If the factor carried by the mixed wall is replaced
by $G_{\mathrm{mix}}$, the product around a small loop about the joint
becomes $R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\pm1}$ instead of the identity, the sign depending
on the orientation of the loop.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cite[Notation~2.40]{grossetal2022open}, the balanced graphs of
$(\{i\},(1))$ have $(k_1,k_2)=(1,4)$ and $(5,0)$, those of $(\{j\},(1))$
have $(0,5)$ and $(4,1)$, and those of $(J,(1,1))$, $J=\{i,j\}$, have
$(1,5)$ and $(5,1)$; the only critical exponent of $J$ is $(1,1)$.  The
chamber index $\nu^{\min}$ can be nonzero only at the pairs $(1,4)$, $(0,5)$
and $(1,5)$ of least $k_1$.  By \eqref{eq:quartic-Gamma-weight}, the
single-label chamber conditions $A(\{i\},(1),\nu^{\min})=
A(\{j\},(1),\nu^{\min})=-1$ read
$\frac14\nu^{\min}_{\{i\},(1,4)}=-1$ and $\frac12\nu^{\min}_{\{j\},(0,5)}=-1$.
The set $J$ has the two partitions $\{J\}$ and $\{\{i\},\{j\}\}$, and
$(1,4)+(0,5)=(1,9)$; since $r_J=s_J=1$, the weights are
$w_J(1,5)=\Gamma(\frac12)\Gamma(\frac32)/\Gamma(\frac12)^2=\frac12$ and
$w_J(1,9)=\Gamma(\frac12)\Gamma(\frac52)/\Gamma(\frac12)^2=\frac34$.  The
chamber condition $A(J,(1,1),\nu^{\min})=0$
\cite[Def.~3.47(3) and Notation~3.42]{grossetal2022open} is therefore
\[
 w_J(1,5)\,\nu^{\min}_{J,(1,5)}
 +w_J(1,9)\,\nu^{\min}_{\{i\},(1,4)}\,\nu^{\min}_{\{j\},(0,5)}
 =\tfrac12\,\nu^{\min}_{J,(1,5)}+\tfrac34\cdot(-4)\cdot(-2)=0,
\]
so $\nu^{\min}_{J,(1,5)}=-12$.  The same computation for $\nu^{\max}$, with
the pairs $(5,0)$, $(4,1)$, $(5,1)$ and $(9,1)$ in place of $(1,4)$, $(0,5)$,
$(1,5)$ and $(1,9)$, gives the single-label values $-2$ and $-4$ and
$\nu^{\max}_{J,(5,1)}=-12$.  By \cite[Def.~4.6]{grossetal2022open} these
invariants, with the signs $(-1)^{|J|-1}$, are the coefficients in
\eqref{eq:mixed-potentials}; in particular the mixed coefficient is
$(-1)^{|J|-1}\nu=12$.

The factor $\Sigma_1$ carries $W^{\min}$ to
$x^4+y^4-2t_1x^5-2t_2y^5+2t_1t_2xy^5$, because
$X_{1,0}(x^4+y^4)=4x^5-8xy^4$ and $X_{1,0}(y^5)=-10xy^5$.  The factor
$\Sigma_2$ does not change the coefficient of $t_1t_2xy^5$, and
\[
 X_{1,1}(x^4+y^4)=8x^5y-8xy^5.
\]
Since $W^{\max}$ has no $t_1t_2xy^5$ term, the mixed GKT generator has
coefficient $2/8=1/4$, proving \eqref{eq:mixed-GKT-transition}.

Let $\Theta_{\mathrm{mix}}$ be the factor carried by the mixed wall.  The
path $\gamma_+$ crosses the $\Sigma_1$-chord, the mixed wall and the
$\Sigma_2$-chord positively and in this order, and $\gamma_-$ crosses the
negative half-chords of $\Sigma_2$ and of $\Sigma_1$ positively and in this
order.  Consistency at the joint (Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency})
therefore requires $\Sigma_2\Theta_{\mathrm{mix}}\Sigma_1=\Sigma_1\Sigma_2$.
Equation \eqref{eq:first-bracket} gives
$\Sigma_2\Sigma_1=Z_{3/4}\Sigma_1\Sigma_2$, and the square-zero mixed ray is
central, so $\Theta_{\mathrm{mix}}=Z_{-3/4}$.  Subtraction of logarithms on
this ray gives $-\frac34-\frac14=-1$, which is \eqref{eq:three-quarter-split}.
Finally, replacing $Z_{-3/4}$ by
$G_{\mathrm{mix}}=Z_{-3/4}R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}$ multiplies the product along
$\gamma_+$ by the central element $R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}$, so the two loop
products are $R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}$ and $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$.
\end{proof}

Thus $R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}=Z_1$ is the correction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport}(3): along $\gamma_+$,
$\Sigma_2G_{\mathrm{mix}}\Sigma_1
=R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}\Sigma_2Z_{-3/4}\Sigma_1$, and the right side is
$R_{\mathrm{mix}}^{-1}$ times the path-ordered product of $\mathfrak D$.  The
GKT factor is not the image of the wall factor under any homomorphism fixing
the incoming factors, since such a homomorphism fixes their commutator
$Z_{-3/4}=\Sigma_2^{-1}\Sigma_1\Sigma_2\Sigma_1^{-1}$.  Nor does an abelian
quotient separate the two factors: a character of a group containing
$\Sigma_1$, $\Sigma_2$ and $G_{\mathrm{mix}}$ is trivial on the commutator
$Z_{-3/4}$ and therefore takes inverse values on $G_{\mathrm{mix}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$.

\subsection{Raywise factorization in all orders}
\label{subsec:raywise-factorization}

Let $\mathcal I$ be a finite set of distinct labels of descendent degree
one, each of twist $\alpha=(1,0)$ or $\beta=(0,1)$.  We write
$t_i=u_{i,1}\in A_{\mathcal I}$, so that $t_i^2=0$, and base-change the walls
of $\mathfrak D$ along
\[
 t_1\longmapsto t_\alpha=\sum_{\tau(i)=\alpha}t_i,
 \qquad t_2\longmapsto t_\beta=\sum_{\tau(i)=\beta}t_i,
\]
as in \eqref{eq:window-map}.  This is the base change for the labelled
truncation $(\mathcal I,\tau,P_{\mathcal I})$ of
Definition~\ref{def:coefficient-window}, with the twists $\alpha,\beta$ read
as $1,2$ and with $P_{\mathcal I}$ the set of square-free monomials in the
$t_i$; we call it the \emph{labelled truncation} of $\mathcal I$.  For
$N>|\mathcal I|$ its coefficient ring is
$\Bbbk[t_i:i\in\mathcal I]/(t_i^2:i\in\mathcal I)$, the base change to
$\Bbbk$ of the subring of $A_{\mathcal I}$ generated by the $t_i$.  For
$\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\mathcal I$ put
\[
 p_J=|J_\alpha|,
 \qquad q_J=|J_\beta|,
 \qquad t_J=\prod_{i\in J}t_i,
\]
where $J_\alpha$ and $J_\beta$ are the labels in $J$ of twist $\alpha$ and
$\beta$, and write $p_J=r_J+4\ell_J$, $q_J=s_J+4m_J$ with $0\leq
r_J,s_J\leq3$ as in Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}.  By
\cite[Notation~2.40 and (2.30)--(2.31)]{grossetal2022open}, the pairs
$(k_1(\Gamma),k_2(\Gamma))$ of the balanced graphs $\Gamma$ of $J$, which we
call the \emph{balanced profiles} of $J$, and the critical exponents of $J$,
are
\begin{align}
 b_{J,h}
 &=(r_J+4h,s_J+4(\ell_J+m_J+1-h)),
 &&0\leq h\leq\ell_J+m_J+1,\label{eq:balanced-profiles}\\
 k_{J,h}
 &=(r_J+4h,s_J+4(\ell_J+m_J-h)),
 &&0\leq h\leq\ell_J+m_J.\label{eq:critical-profiles}
\end{align}
Write
\[
 b_J^{\min}=b_{J,0},
 \qquad
 b_J^{\max}=b_{J,\ell_J+m_J+1}.
\]
Among the balanced graphs of support $t_J$, the extremal chamber index
$\nu^\epsilon$, $\epsilon\in\{\min,\max\}$, is nonzero at most at the graph
with $(k_1,k_2)=b_J^\epsilon$; we write $\nu_J^\epsilon$ for its coordinate there and call it
the \emph{extremal invariant} of $J$.  For two labels of twists $\alpha$ and
$\beta$ the extremal GKT potentials are \eqref{eq:mixed-potentials}.

In the labelled truncation of $\mathcal I$ every label has descendent degree
one, so the inequality in
the third chamber condition holds for every $J$, and the chamber conditions
read $A(J,\mathbf d,\nu)=-1$ for $|J|=1$ and $A(J,\mathbf d,\nu)=0$ for
$|J|\geq2$.  Since $\nu^\epsilon$ has on each block $C$ of a partition only
the coordinate at $b_C^\epsilon$, the formula for $A$ in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections} turns these conditions into the
\emph{extremal recursion}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-recursion}
 w_J(b_J^\epsilon)\nu_J^\epsilon
 +\sum_{\substack{\pi\in\operatorname{Part}(J)\\|\pi|\geq2}}
 w_J\!\left(\sum_{C\in\pi}b_C^\epsilon\right)
 \prod_{C\in\pi}\nu_C^\epsilon
 =\begin{cases}-1,&|J|=1,\\0,&|J|\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $w_J(b_J^\epsilon)$ of $\nu_J^\epsilon$ is nonzero, so the
recursion determines the extremal invariants by induction on $|J|$.

On the scattering side, the path $\gamma_+$ crosses positively the positive
half-chord of least slope, which carries $e^{\mathsf A-C_{\mathfrak r_{2,1}}}$,
then the outgoing walls in increasing shifted slope, the wall of $\mathfrak r$
carrying $e^{-C_{\mathfrak r}}$, and then the positive half-chord of greatest
slope, which carries $e^{\mathsf B-C_{\mathfrak r_{1,2}}}$.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:annular-consistency} its path-ordered product equals that of
$\gamma_-$, which crosses the negative half-chords, carrying $e^{\mathsf B}$
and $e^{\mathsf A}$; this is \eqref{eq:singleton-increasing-slope}.  Grouping
the factors by shifted ray gives, for every $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increasing-slope-factorization}
 e^{\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}
 =\overleftarrow{\prod_{\mathfrak r}}
 \exp\Bigl(\sum_{\kappa+(p,q)\in\mathfrak r}a_{p,q}E_{p,q}\Bigr)
 \pmod{\mathfrak m^N},
\end{equation}
where $\overleftarrow{\prod}$ places the factor of least slope on the right,
so that it acts first.  By Proposition~\ref{prop:singleton-completion}(3) this
factorization is unique, and $a_{p,q}$ is the coefficient of $E_{p,q}$ in the
logarithm of the factor carried by the wall of $\mathfrak D$ through $O_0$
whose factor lies in the ray group of the ray of $\kappa+(p,q)$; on a chord
ray this is the positive half-chord.  Thus $a_{1,0}=a_{0,1}=-\frac12$ are the
chord factors, $a_{p,0}=a_{0,q}=0$ for $p,q\geq2$, and $a_{p,q}=-c_{p,q}$ for
$p,q\geq1$, where $c_{p,q}$ are the coefficients of
Proposition~\ref{prop:singleton-completion} and
Table~\ref{tab:singleton-coefficients}.  In particular $a_{p,q}=a_{q,p}$,
and the first values are
\[
 a_{1,1}=-\tfrac34,
 \qquad a_{2,1}=a_{1,2}=-\tfrac38,
 \qquad a_{3,1}=a_{1,3}=-\tfrac1{16},
 \qquad a_{2,2}=-\tfrac38.
\]

\begin{definition}[GKT, wall and residual logarithms]
\label{def:raywise-logarithms}
In the labelled truncation of $\mathcal I$, let $\mathfrak r$ be a shifted ray.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The \emph{GKT logarithm} $G_{\mathfrak r}$ is the logarithm of the GKT
factor on $\mathfrak r$ of the GKT transition from $\nu^{\min}$ to
$\nu^{\max}$.
\item The \emph{wall logarithm} $S_{\mathfrak r}$ is the logarithm of the
base change of the factor on $\mathfrak r$ in
\eqref{eq:increasing-slope-factorization}, that is, of the factor carried by
the wall of $\mathfrak D$ on $\mathfrak r$ (on a chord ray, by its positive
half) in the labelled truncation; $S_{\mathfrak r}=0$ if no wall carries a factor
in the ray group of $\mathfrak r$.
\item The \emph{residual logarithm} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-ray}
 R_{\mathfrak r}=S_{\mathfrak r}-G_{\mathfrak r},
\end{equation}
and $\exp(R_{\mathfrak r})$ is the \emph{residual factor} on $\mathfrak r$.
\end{enumerate}
For $J\subseteq\mathcal I$ we write $G_J$, $S_J$ and $R_J$ for the sums over
$\mathfrak r$ of the $t_J$-components of $G_{\mathfrak r}$, $S_{\mathfrak r}$
and $R_{\mathfrak r}$.  By \eqref{eq:critical-profiles},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:GKT-logarithm-J}
 G_J=t_J\sum_hg_{J,h}X_{k_{J,h}}
\end{equation}
for rational coefficients $g_{J,h}$.
\end{definition}

The generators on one shifted ray commute, so \eqref{eq:residual-ray}
exponentiates to $\exp(S_{\mathfrak r})=\exp(G_{\mathfrak r})
\exp(R_{\mathfrak r})$.  The product of the factors $\exp(G_{\mathfrak r})$,
with least slope acting first, is the GKT transition, which carries
$W^{\nu^{\min}}$ to $W^{\nu^{\max}}$, and the product of the factors
$\exp(S_{\mathfrak r})$ is the base change of
\eqref{eq:increasing-slope-factorization}, the path-ordered product across the
joint in the labelled truncation.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decorated-word}
 \overleftarrow{\prod_{\mathfrak r}}\exp(S_{\mathfrak r})
 =\overleftarrow{\prod_{\mathfrak r}}
  \bigl(\exp(G_{\mathfrak r})\exp(R_{\mathfrak r})\bigr),
\end{equation}
in which each factor on the left is replaced, in its own position, by two
commuting factors in the same ray group.  On a ray that carries no wall,
$S_{\mathfrak r}=0$ and $R_{\mathfrak r}=-G_{\mathfrak r}$, so the right side
of \eqref{eq:decorated-word} contains the pair
$\exp(G_{\mathfrak r})\exp(-G_{\mathfrak r})$; the factorization adds no wall
to $\mathfrak D$.

\begin{proposition}[Raywise factorization in all orders]
\label{thm:all-order-decoration}
In the labelled truncation of every finite set $\mathcal I$ of descendent-one
labels of twists $\alpha$ and $\beta$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the extremal invariants determine the coefficients $g_{J,h}$ in
\eqref{eq:GKT-logarithm-J} uniquely, support by support in increasing order
of $|J|$;
\item the full-support wall logarithm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scattering-logarithm-J}
 S_J=p_J!\,q_J!\,a_{p_J,q_J}t_JX_{p_J,q_J};
\end{equation}
\item the residual logarithms vanish on singleton supports:
$G_J=S_J=-\frac12t_iX_{\tau(i)}$ and $R_J=0$ for $J=\{i\}$;
\item the factorization \eqref{eq:decorated-word} is natural under
relabelling, deletion of labels, restriction of finite labelled systems
closed under the dependencies of Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion},
reduction of deformation order, and Jacobi transport along admissible paths (Section~\ref{subsec:cofactor-supports}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The GKT dimension formula gives
\eqref{eq:balanced-profiles}--\eqref{eq:critical-profiles}.  The critical
generators act on the balanced monomials of one support as the weighted
incidence matrix of the path graph with vertices
$b_{J,0},\dots,b_{J,\ell_J+m_J+1}$.  Indeed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-incidence}
 X_{u,v}(x^4+y^4)
 =4(v+1)x^{u+4}y^v-4(u+1)x^uy^{v+4}.
\end{equation}
For $(u,v)=k_{J,h}$ the two monomials in
\eqref{eq:critical-incidence} are precisely the adjacent vertices
$b_{J,h+1}$ and $b_{J,h}$.  The resulting path incidence matrix has full
column rank, and its one left-kernel relation is the chamber equation, which
the difference of two chamber indices satisfies
\cite[Cor.~4.28 and (5.50)]{grossetal2022open}.  Hence the coefficients
$g_{J,h}$ exist and are unique, support by support in increasing order of
$|J|$.  For $J=\{i\}$ this gives $G_J=-\frac12t_iX_{\tau(i)}$, as in
\eqref{eq:mixed-GKT-transition}.

The base change carries $e^{\mathsf A}e^{\mathsf B}$ to the path-ordered
product in the labelled truncation and each ray factor of
\eqref{eq:increasing-slope-factorization} to a labelled ray factor.  Since
$t_i^2=0$, the coefficient of $t_J$ in $t_\alpha^{p_J}t_\beta^{q_J}$ is
$p_J!q_J!$, and no other monomial $t_\alpha^pt_\beta^q$ contains $t_J$; this
gives \eqref{eq:scattering-logarithm-J}.  For $J=\{i\}$ it gives
$S_J=-\frac12t_iX_{\tau(i)}=G_J$, so the singleton residuals vanish.  Both
constructions are triangular in proper subsets, proving naturality under
restriction; the other naturality statements are immediate from the
constructions.
\end{proof}

For the mixed pair $J=\{i,j\}$, the logarithms $G_J$, $S_J$ and $R_J$ have
coefficients $\frac14$, $-\frac34$ and $-1$ on $t_JX_{1,1}$, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:first-decoration}.  For $J$ of type $(2\alpha,\beta)$
or $(\alpha,2\beta)$ the only critical exponent is $k=(2,1)$ or $(1,2)$, and
in both cases $G_J$, $S_J$ and $R_J$ have coefficients $-\frac12$, $-\frac34$
and $-\frac14$ on $t_JX_k$.  For two labels $i,i'$ of twist $\alpha$, the GKT
transition contains $\exp(\frac12t_it_{i'}X_{2,0})$ on the shifted ray of
$(3,1)$, which lies outside the cone spanned by $(2,1)$ and $(1,2)$ and
carries no wall; there $R_{\mathfrak r}=-G_{\mathfrak r}$.

The identity $-\frac34=\frac14-1$ concerns only the coefficient of
$t_1t_2X_{1,1}$ on the first mixed ray $\RR_{\geq0}\kappa$.  This ray also
contains the image $X_\kappa^\kappa$ of the degree-zero generator.  If labels
of twist $(2,2)$ and descendent degree zero are admitted, the GKT transition
has on this ray the degree-zero component $\exp(fX_{0,0})$, where
$f=\frac1{32}t_{2,2}^2+O(t_{2,2}^4)$ in the symmetric variable $t_{2,2}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:primary-frobenius}; this component survives on the primary
slice, where all descendent variables vanish.  Every wall of $\mathfrak D$ is
congruent to the identity modulo $\mathfrak m=(t_1,t_2)$
(Definition~\ref{def:Jacobi-wall}), so the diagram has no wall on the primary
slice, and the residual component there is $\exp(-fX_{0,0})$.

\begin{remark}[Products along different paths]
\label{rem:products-different-paths}
The comparison extends from wall factors to transport along paths.  Along a
path transverse to the walls, the transport of functions through
$\mathfrak D$ and the \emph{GKT transport}, obtained from it by replacing
every wall factor with the GKT factor on its ray, differ by one automorphism
of the target chart algebra, the \emph{residual transport} of the path.
Products of functions carried along the same path are therefore related by
this automorphism.  For functions carried along different paths the residual
transports differ in general: for the paths $\gamma_+$ and $\gamma_-$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:two-label-transport}, which have the same endpoints,
they are $Z_{-1}$ and the identity.  Appendix~\ref{app:residual-coherence}
corrects each factor by a degree-weighted interpolation of the residual
transports, so that the corrected product is the image of the product of
the GKT transports under one automorphism of the target algebra
(Lemma~\ref{prop:residual-midpoint} and
Proposition~\ref{thm:residual-coherence}).  The corrected products for
different parenthesizations are related by automorphisms that satisfy the
cocycle identity; for three factors the automorphism relating the two
parenthesizations is not the identity in general
(Proposition~\ref{thm:residual-coherence}).  Changes of the paths act
compatibly on the corrected products
(Proposition~\ref{prop:corridor-groupoid}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Compatible homotopies of canonical multisections}

A \emph{GKT graph datum} is a graded open $(4,4)$-graph $\Gamma$ together
with a descendent vector $\mathbf d$ on its internal labels; its support is
the monomial $u_{I(\Gamma),\mathbf d}$.  A \emph{finite dependency-closed
partial system} $\mathbb D=(I,P,\mathcal E)$ records a finite twist-labelled
set $I$, a finite set $P$ of labelled monomials closed under divisors, and a
finite set $\mathcal E$ of graph data whose supports lie in $P$.  The graph
data are closed under the connected-component and boundary dependencies of
the assembling relation \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}, and, for every support in
the monomial set that carries a balanced graph, they contain all graph data
of that support whose graph is balanced or critical; the monomials and the
graph data are invariant under twist-preserving permutations of the labels.
The precise definition is
Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}, and
Appendix~\ref{app:gkt-completion} proves the following theorem by relative
extension arguments.

\begin{theorem}[Compatible GKT homotopies]
\label{thm:compatible-GKT-realization}
There are finite dependency-closed partial systems
$\mathbb D_n=(I_n,P_n,\mathcal E_n)$, $n\geq1$, with
$\mathbb D_1\subset\mathbb D_2\subset\cdots$, exhausting all GKT graph data,
compatible canonical families realizing the two extremal chamber indices,
and a componentwise smooth homotopy of canonical multisections between them,
smooth on each $[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the countably many ray factors occur in pairwise disjoint open time
intervals, ordered by increasing slope;
\item each fixed component is nonconstant on only finitely many such
intervals and is constant near the ends of each of them;
\item for every $n$, modulo the monomials of $A_{I_n}$ outside $P_n$, the
potential at the right end of the interval of a ray is the image of the
potential at its left end under the GKT factor on that ray, that is, under
the factor on that ray of the slope factorization
\cite[Thm.~1.2]{wuebben2026boundary} of the GKT transition from
$\nu^{\min}$ to $\nu^{\max}$, and only finitely many of these factors are
nontrivial; and
\item the restrictions to the $\mathbb D_n$ are compatible under the
restriction maps of Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}, and the
families and the homotopy are the inverse limit of these restrictions over
the inverse system $\mathbb D_1\subset\mathbb D_2\subset\cdots$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

On one interval of (1) and one finite system, the homotopy is a simple
canonical homotopy in the normal form of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}
for its own assignment of times to label subsets
(Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-homotopy-lifting}).  Thus every GKT factor in
Proposition~\ref{thm:all-order-decoration} is the jump of a finite
restriction of one compatible homotopy.
