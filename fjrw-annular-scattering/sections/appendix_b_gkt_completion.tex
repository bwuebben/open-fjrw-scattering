\section{Compatible completion of GKT homotopies}
\label{app:gkt-completion}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization}, the
compatibility statement in Part~(3) of Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-comparison}.
The labels and monomials of a labelled truncation
(Definition~\ref{def:coefficient-window}) do not by themselves define a
restriction map on canonical families: by the assembling relation
\eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}, the multisection on one graph datum is prescribed
on the boundary of its moduli space by the multisections on the connected
components of the bases of its boundary graphs.  We therefore restrict along
finite systems of graph data closed under this relation, the finite
assembling-closed systems of Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system}.

We use the notions of \cite{grossetal2022open} recalled in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}.  Fix a countable set
$I_\infty$ of labels with a twist map, containing infinitely many labels
of each twist.  Every construction below uses only a finite subset.  The
relative extension argument is the multisection argument of
\cite[Lem.~6.5]{buryaketal2024} in its $W$-spin form
\cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open}; these statements, the homotopy lemma
\cite[Lem.~5.17]{grossetal2022open}, and the part of the proof of
\cite[Thm.~5.13]{grossetal2022open} that we use are recalled in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-relative-extension}.  The lemma of GKT that
provides simple canonical homotopies with assigned times,
\cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}, is recalled below together with the
parts of its proof that we use.  From
\cite{wuebben2026boundary} we use only the extremal chamber indices
\cite[Thm.~1.1]{wuebben2026boundary}, the freeness of the enlarged action
\cite[Lem.~2.5]{wuebben2026boundary}, and the slope factorization
\cite[Prop.~3.1]{wuebben2026boundary}.

\subsection{Finite assembling-closed systems}

For a graded open $(4,4)$-graph $\Gamma$ with finite internal label set and
descendent vector $\mathbf d$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-GKT-datum}
 \delta=(\Gamma,\mathbf d),
 \qquad
 E_\delta=E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)\longrightarrow\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(\delta)=u_{I(\Gamma),\mathbf d}.
\end{equation}
We call $\delta$ a \emph{graph datum}.  A family of canonical
multisections satisfies the assembling relation \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling} on
every zero or exchange boundary stratum $\Lambda$ of $\Gamma$.  The
\emph{assembling constituents} of $\delta$ are the connected component data
of $B\Gamma$ and of all $B\Lambda$ occurring there, with restricted
descendent vectors; by \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}, their multisections
prescribe the multisection of $\delta$ on the boundary.  The
\emph{assembling closure} of a set of graph data consists of these data and
their iterated assembling constituents.

\begin{definition}\label{def:finite-partial-system}
A \emph{finite assembling-closed system} is a triple
$\mathbb D=(I,\mathscr P,\mathcal E)$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I\subset I_\infty$ is finite and twist labelled; $\mathscr P$ is a finite
divisor-closed set of labelled monomials, containing $1$ and invariant under
twist-preserving permutations of $I$;
\item $\mathcal E$ is a finite set of graph data containing every iterated
assembling constituent and connected component datum of each of its elements;
\item $\operatorname{supp}(\delta)\in\mathscr P$ for every $\delta\in\mathcal E$;
\item if a support $u\in\mathscr P$ carries a balanced graph, then $\mathcal E$
contains every graph datum of support $u$ whose graph is balanced or
critical, together with its assembling closure;
\item $\mathcal E$ is invariant under the twist-preserving permutation group of $I$.
\end{enumerate}
An inclusion $\mathbb D\subset\mathbb D'$ means inclusion of the label sets,
of the monomial sets and of the sets of graph data.  Restriction is
therefore the forgetful map.
\end{definition}

In Theorems~\ref{thm:relative-endpoint-lifting}
and~\ref{thm:relative-homotopy-lifting} below, the smaller system may also
be the \emph{empty system}, with no labels, monomials or graph data; then
there are no old data, and the two theorems are
\cite[Thm.~5.13]{grossetal2022open} and the construction of
\cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}, with the time collars and the corner
modification described below.

\begin{lemma}[Finite closure]\label{lem:finite-GKT-closure}
The assembling closure of a finite set of graph data, together with all
balanced and critical graph data of finitely many supports, is finite.
Moreover, if $\delta'$ lies in the assembling closure of $\delta$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-divisibility}
 \operatorname{supp}(\delta')\mid\operatorname{supp}(\delta).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An assembling constituent uses a subset of the internal labels and the
restricted descendent vector, and iteration proves \eqref{eq:support-divisibility}.  Order connected
graphs lexicographically by
\[
 \bigl(|I(\Gamma)|,n(\Gamma),-k_{12}(\Gamma)\bigr),
 \qquad n=4k_1+4k_2+8k_{12}.
\]
Every proper connected base component precedes the original graph.  There is
no infinite descending chain, and every graph has finitely many boundary
types.  Thus the finitely branching tree of iterated assembling constituents is
finite.  A support
has finitely many balanced and critical graph data
\cite[Notation~2.40]{grossetal2022open}, and permutation orbits are finite.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal C(\mathbb D)$ be the collections of transverse, strongly
positive, automorphism-invariant canonical multisections on $\mathcal E$
satisfying \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}.  Independent choices are made only on
graph data whose graph is smooth and connected; the other data are the
pullbacks and external sums prescribed by GKT.  A chamber index, or a
family of canonical multisections, is \emph{symmetric} in the sense of
\cite[Notation~3.42 and Def.~3.43]{grossetal2022open} if it is invariant
under the twist-preserving permutations of the labels, which act on families
through their natural lifts to the moduli spaces.

We write $\mathfrak t\in[0,1]$ for the time of a homotopy.  For $r=s=4$, the
lemma of GKT on simple canonical homotopies reads as follows; for a graph
$\Gamma$ and a descendent vector $\mathbf d$ we abbreviate
$\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\operatorname{rank}E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$ and
$\dim\Gamma=\dim\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}}]
Fix a set $I$ of labels, and let $\mathfrak s^0$ and $\mathfrak s^1$ be
families of transverse canonical multisections bounded by $I$.  Then there
are a family $H$ of simple canonical homotopies from $\mathfrak s^0$ to
$\mathfrak s^1$, bounded by $I$, and pairwise distinct times
$\mathfrak t(A)\in(0,1)$, one for each $A\subseteq I$, with the following
properties for every smooth graph $\Gamma$ with only open vertices and
$I(\Gamma)\subseteq I$ and every descendent vector $\mathbf d$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The homotopy $H^\Gamma$ on $\Gamma$ is nowhere zero if (a) $\Gamma$ is
irrelevant; or (b) $\Gamma$ is connected and
$\operatorname{rk}\Gamma\geq\dim\Gamma+2$; or (c) $\Gamma$ contains two connected
components $\Xi_1,\Xi_2$ with $I(\Xi_1)\neq I(\Xi_2)$ such that
$\operatorname{rk}\Xi_j=\dim\Xi_j+1$ and $k_{12}(\Xi_j)\leq2$ for $j=1,2$.
\item If $\Gamma$ is connected and either (a)
$\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma+1$ and $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq2$, or (b)
$\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma$ and $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$, then
$H^\Gamma$ is transverse to zero on $[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ and on
$[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda$ for every
$\Lambda\in\partial^0\Gamma\cup\partial^{\mathrm{exchange}}\Gamma$.
\item Let $\Gamma$ be connected.  (a) If
$\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma+1$ and $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq2$, then
$H^\Gamma$ is nowhere zero on
$([0,1]\setminus\{\mathfrak t(I(\Gamma))\})\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$.  (b)
If $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma$ and $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$, then for
every $A\subseteq I$ the restriction of $H^\Gamma$ to
$\{\mathfrak t(A)\}\times(\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma\setminus\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma)$
is transverse to zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{importedthm}

The proof constructs $H^\Gamma$ for smooth connected graphs by induction on
$\dim\Gamma$; on disconnected graphs the homotopy is determined by
simplicity, and (1)(c) follows from (3)(a).  The boundary values are, on a
stratum $\Lambda$ with a contracted boundary half-edge, the linear homotopy
${(1-\mathfrak t)s^0_\Lambda+\mathfrak ts^1_\Lambda}$ between the components
$s^0_\Lambda$ and $s^1_\Lambda$ of $\mathfrak s^0$ and $\mathfrak s^1$, and on the other strata
the assembled homotopy $F_\Lambda^*\bigl(\boxplus_\Xi H^\Xi\bigr)$ over the
connected components $\Xi$ of $B\Lambda$.  On irrelevant boundary strata the
boundary values are nowhere zero, inductively by (1)(a).  For a relevant
connected graph $\Gamma$ the proof distinguishes three cases.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma\geq\dim\Gamma+2$, the boundary values
are nowhere zero, and a transverse extension is nowhere zero.
\item[(b)] If $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma+1$ and
$k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq2$, a dimension count shows that the base of every
relevant boundary stratum has a component $\Xi$ with
$\operatorname{rk}\Xi\geq\dim\Xi+2$, or two components $\Xi$ with
$\operatorname{rk}\Xi=\dim\Xi+1$, distinct label sets and
$k_{12}(\Xi)\leq2$, so that the
boundary values are nowhere zero by (1)(b) and (1)(c) for the base.  A
transverse extension has finitely many zeros; after a small perturbation no
two of them lie over the same point of $\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$, and a
diffeomorphism of
$[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ that commutes with the projection to
$\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ and is the identity on
$[0,1]\times\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ and near the positive boundary
moves them to the time $\mathfrak t(I(\Gamma))$.
\item[(c)] If $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma=\dim\Gamma$ and
$k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$, then on every relevant boundary stratum either the
boundary values are nowhere zero or their transversality follows from
(1)--(3) for the components of the base.  For a base with a
component $\Xi_1$ with $\operatorname{rk}\Xi_1=\dim\Xi_1$ and a component
$\Xi_2$ with $\operatorname{rk}\Xi_2=\dim\Xi_2+1$, it uses the
transversality of the slice of $H^{\Xi_1}$ at the time
$\mathfrak t(I(\Xi_2))$, the only time at which $H^{\Xi_2}$ vanishes.  Near
each boundary zero the data are extended so that their restriction to the
interior slice through it is transverse; the data are then made transverse
on the slices at the times $\mathfrak t(A)$ and extended transversally.
\end{enumerate}
On the remaining relevant connected graphs the homotopy is extended
arbitrarily.

For homotopies, fix $\varepsilon\in(0,\frac12)$ and a nondecreasing smooth collar function
$c:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ with $c=0$ on $[0,\varepsilon]$ and $c=1$ on
$[1-\varepsilon,1]$, and fix distinct times
$\mathfrak t(J)\in(\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)$ for the subsets
$J\subseteq I$.  We call $[0,\varepsilon]$ and $[1-\varepsilon,1]$ the
\emph{time collars}; the homotopies below are constant in time on them, and
the assigned times lie outside them.  Let
$\mathcal H(\mathbb D;\mathfrak s^0,\mathfrak s^1;(\mathfrak t(J))_{J\subseteq
I})$ denote the canonical homotopies on $\mathcal E$ from $\mathfrak s^0$ to
$\mathfrak s^1$ that are simple in the sense of
\cite[Def.~3.36]{grossetal2022open} and satisfy, in addition, the
normal-form conditions of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open} for the times
$\mathfrak t(J)$, under the hypotheses stated there.  Irrelevant components, and
connected components whose rank exceeds the dimension by at least two, are
nowhere zero.  A connected component $\Gamma$ whose rank exceeds the
dimension by one and with $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq2$ is transverse on
$[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ and on every stratum
$[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda$ with
$\Lambda\in\partial^0\Gamma\cup\partial^{\mathrm{exchange}}\Gamma$, and
vanishes only over $\mathfrak t(I(\Gamma))$.  A connected component $\Gamma$
whose rank equals the dimension and with $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$ is transverse
on the same strata and on the slices
$\{\mathfrak t(J)\}\times(\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma\setminus\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma)$
at the assigned times.  Since the times are distinct, the external sum of two
components of the first kind with different internal label sets is nowhere
zero, which is \cite[Lem.~3.40(1)(c)]{grossetal2022open}.  No condition is imposed on the other relevant
components.  The critical graphs ($k_{12}=0$) and the balanced graphs
($k_{12}=1$), which are the graphs entering the wall-crossing formulas, are
covered by these conditions.

Collars are part of the data.  A \emph{collar} of a multisection or a
homotopy along a closed subset of its domain, namely a graph boundary
stratum, the positive boundary, or one of the ends $\mathfrak t=0$ and
$\mathfrak t=1$, is a fixed germ of it along that subset.  Along the ends, a
homotopy from $\mathfrak s^0$ to $\mathfrak s^1$ equals $\mathfrak s^0$,
respectively $\mathfrak s^1$, on the time collars.  Along a graph boundary
stratum the germ is not required to be constant in the normal direction:
near an isolated boundary zero of a component whose rank equals the
dimension, it is the extension into the interior used in the proof of
\cite[Lem.~3.40(3)(b)]{grossetal2022open}, for which the interior slice
through that zero is transverse.  A germ constant in the normal coordinate
would make this slice contain a segment of zeros.

\subsection{Relative extension}
\label{subsec:gkt-relative-extension}

We first recall the extension and modification results on which the
constructions below rest.  In them, $\mathcal{PM}$ has the meaning of
Subsection~\ref{subsec:gkt-recollections}; it is the space written
$\overline{\mathcal{PM}}$ in \cite{grossetal2022open} and
\cite{buryaketal2024}.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open}}]
Let $\Gamma$ be a smooth connected open graded $W$-spin graph, and let
$\zeta$ be a multisection of $E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$ defined over
$\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$.  Suppose that, for every
$\Lambda\in\partial\Gamma\setminus\partial^+\Gamma$,
\[
 \zeta|_{\mathcal{PM}_\Lambda}=F_\Lambda^*\zeta^{B\Lambda},
\]
where $\zeta^{B\Lambda}$ is a transverse multisection of
$E_{B\Lambda}(\mathbf d)$ over $\mathcal{PM}_{B\Lambda}$ that is strongly
positive with respect to $B\Lambda$ and $\operatorname{Aut}(B\Gamma)$-invariant.
Then $\zeta$ extends to a transverse, strongly positive,
$\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$-invariant multisection of $E_\Gamma(\mathbf d)$.
\end{importedthm}

The statement also holds when $\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ is empty
\cite[Rem.~5.16]{grossetal2022open}, and its proof is that of the following
$r$-spin statement of Buryak, Clader and Tessler, whose first two items we
use; the Witten bundle there is that of graded $r$-spin disks.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[Lem.~6.5(1)--(2)]{buryaketal2024}}]
Let $\Gamma$ be a smooth, connected, open, graded $r$-spin dual graph, and let
$\zeta$ be a multisection of the Witten bundle over
$\bigcup_{\Lambda\in\partial\Gamma\setminus\partial^+\Gamma}
\mathcal M^{1/r}_\Lambda$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Suppose that, for every
$\Lambda\in\partial\Gamma\setminus\partial^+\Gamma$,
$\zeta|_{\mathcal{PM}^{1/r}_\Lambda}=F_\Lambda^*\zeta^{B\Lambda}$ for a
multisection $\zeta^{B\Lambda}$ of the Witten bundle over
$\mathcal{PM}^{1/r}_{B\Lambda}$ that is positive with respect to $B\Lambda$,
$\operatorname{Aut}(B\Gamma)$-invariant and transverse.  Then $\zeta$ extends
to a positive, transverse, $\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)$-invariant multisection
$\sigma$ of the Witten bundle over $\mathcal{PM}^{1/r}_\Gamma$.
\item Suppose, in addition, that for every
$\Lambda\in\partial\Gamma\setminus\partial^+\Gamma$ such that $B\Lambda$ has
at least one vertex without boundary tails, the restriction of $\zeta$ to
$\mathcal M^{1/r}_\Lambda$ is strongly positive in the sense of
\cite[Def.~6.1]{buryaketal2024}.  Then $\sigma$ can be chosen to be strongly
positive on $\mathcal{PM}^{1/r}_\Gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{importedthm}

The next lemma of GKT prescribes the zero count of a homotopy; we write its
time as $\mathfrak t$.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[Lem.~5.17]{grossetal2022open}}]
Let $E\to Y$ be an oriented orbifold bundle over an oriented orbifold $Y$
with corners, such that the generic isotropy of $E\to Y$ is trivial and
$\operatorname{rank}_\RR E=\dim_\RR Y+1$.  Let $s$ be a nowhere vanishing
multisection of $E$, and let $c\in\QQ$.  Then there are a multisection $s'$
of $E$ that agrees with $s$ outside a compact subset of the interior of $Y$,
and a transverse homotopy $H:[0,1]\times Y\to E$ with $H(0,-)=s$,
$H(1,-)=s'$ and $H(\mathfrak t,y)=s(y)$ for every $\mathfrak t$ and every
$y\in Y$ outside that compact subset, such that the rational signed count of zeros of
$H$ is $c$.
\end{importedthm}

\begin{lemma}[Equivariant relative multisection extension]
\label{lem:relative-multisection}
Let $\delta=(\Gamma,\mathbf d)$ be a graph datum with $\Gamma$ smooth and
connected, and let $Y$ be either
$\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ or $[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$.  Suppose
compatible invariant data are prescribed on a neighborhood of a collared
invariant closed set $K_0\subset Y$, where $K_0$ contains every collar on
which data are prescribed: the time collars, the collars along the graph
boundary strata and along the positive boundary, and those around the data
already constructed on a smaller system and around the slices at the
assigned times.  If the prescribed data are strongly positive and already
satisfy the required transversality or nonvanishing conditions on $K_0$,
they extend to an invariant global multisection that coincides with them
near $K_0$ and satisfies the same open conditions.  A nowhere-vanishing critical section may additionally be
modified in the smooth interior through a transverse homotopy with
prescribed rational signed zero count.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The argument of \cite[Lem.~6.5]{buryaketal2024}, in the $W$-spin form
\cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open}, extends assembling data prescribed on
the boundary strata $\Lambda\in\partial\Gamma\setminus\partial^+\Gamma$
to a strongly positive multisection near these strata and near the positive
boundary $\partial^+\Gamma$; we include the time collars and the collars
around the slices at the assigned times in the prescribed data.  On the
compact complement choose finitely many compactly supported local
multisections whose branches span every fiber.  Relative parametric
transversality, applied to the finitely many graph strata and slices, gives
a generic linear combination with the required properties.  The
perturbation vanishes near $K_0$, hence leaves the data on all prescribed
collars unchanged and preserves strong positivity.

Perform the construction on the \emph{injectively labelled cover} of $Y$,
the corresponding space of disks whose $k$ boundary markings are numbered
bijectively by $\{1,\dots,k\}$, as in the labelled moduli spaces of
\cite[Def.~2.2]{grossetal2022open}, multiplied by $[0,1]$ if
$Y=[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$; it maps onto $Y$ with finite fibers by
forgetting this numbering.  Then take the normalized union of all finite
automorphism translates.  On $K_0$ every
translate is the same invariant prescribed multisection, so the result
coincides with it there.  The generic smooth interior of the injectively
labelled cover has trivial isotropy.  On this locus,
\cite[Lem.~5.17]{grossetal2022open} supplies a compactly supported critical
modification whose transverse homotopy has any prescribed rational signed
zero count.  Relative transversality preserves that count and leaves the
prescribed collars unchanged.
\end{proof}

\emph{Completion to all graph data.}  Every $\mathfrak s\in\mathcal
C(\mathbb D)$ extends, without change on $\mathcal E$, to transverse,
strongly positive, invariant canonical multisections on all graph data with
labels in $I$.  Indeed, process the missing data with smooth connected graphs
in the order of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-GKT-closure}.  Assembling from data already
constructed prescribes boundary values, which
Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-multisection} extends; the other data are the forced
pullbacks and external sums.  No invariant at a balanced graph datum is
prescribed in this step.

The realization of prescribed invariants at balanced graph data, that is, of
prescribed coordinates of a chamber index, follows the proof of the
realization theorem of GKT.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[Thm.~5.13]{grossetal2022open}}]
Every chamber index bounded by $I$ is realized as the vector
$\nu^{\mathfrak s}$ of open invariants of some canonical family $\mathfrak s$
of multisections bounded by $I$.
\end{importedthm}

The proof is an induction on the number of internal labels.  For a set $J$
of labels and a descendent vector $\mathbf d$ on $J$, let
$\Gamma_{J,0},\dots,\Gamma_{J,N}$ be the balanced graphs of $(J,\mathbf d)$,
with $k_1(\Gamma_{J,p})=r_J+4p$, and let $\Lambda_{J,1},\dots,\Lambda_{J,N}$
be its critical graphs, with $k_1(\Lambda_{J,p})=r_J+4(p-1)+1$
\cite[Notation~2.40]{grossetal2022open}.  The inductive step rests on the
following two assertions, stated here for $r=s=4$.

\begin{importedthm}[{\cite[proof of Thm.~5.13, (5.50) and
(5.52)--(5.54)]{grossetal2022open}}]
Let $\nu$ be a chamber index bounded by $I$, let $J\subseteq I$ and
$\mathbf d\in\ZZ^J_{\geq0}$, and let $\mathfrak s$ be a transverse
canonical family bounded by $J$ such that $\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma,\mathbf
d'}=\nu_{\Gamma,\mathbf d'}$ for every balanced graph datum
$(\Gamma,\mathbf d')$ whose support is a proper divisor of
$u_{J,\mathbf d}$.  Then, with the weight
\eqref{eq:quartic-Gamma-weight},
\[
 \sum_{\Gamma\in\operatorname{Balanced}(J,\mathbf d)}
 w_J\bigl(k_1(\Gamma),k_2(\Gamma)\bigr)
 \bigl(\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}-\nu_{\Gamma,\mathbf d}\bigr)
 =0.
\]
For $1\leq p\leq N$, replace the multisection of $\Lambda_{J,p}$ by one that
agrees with it near the boundary of $\overline{\mathcal
M}{}^W_{\Lambda_{J,p}}$ and is joined to it by a transverse homotopy,
constant in time near that boundary, with rational zero count
$h_{p,\mathbf d}$, and redefine by \cite[Lem.~5.15]{grossetal2022open} the
multisections of the graphs that have a zero or exchange boundary graph
$\Lambda$ with $\Lambda_{J,p}$ a connected component of $B\Lambda$.  Then the
invariants of balanced graphs with fewer than $|J|$ labels do not change, and
the invariant $o'_{J,p,\mathbf d}$ of $\Gamma_{J,p}$ becomes
\begin{align*}
 o'_{J,0,\mathbf d}
 &=\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma_{J,0},\mathbf d}
  +(-1)^{k_2(\Lambda_{J,1})}\,4k_1(\Lambda_{J,1})\,h_{1,\mathbf d},\\
 o'_{J,p,\mathbf d}
 &=\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma_{J,p},\mathbf d}
  +(-1)^{k_2(\Lambda_{J,p})+1}\,4k_2(\Lambda_{J,p})\,h_{p,\mathbf d}\\
 &\qquad+(-1)^{k_2(\Lambda_{J,p+1})}\,4k_1(\Lambda_{J,p+1})\,h_{p+1,\mathbf d}
  \qquad(0<p<N),\\
 o'_{J,N,\mathbf d}
 &=\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma_{J,N},\mathbf d}
  +(-1)^{k_2(\Lambda_{J,N})+1}\,4k_2(\Lambda_{J,N})\,h_{N,\mathbf d}.
\end{align*}
\end{importedthm}

In \cite{grossetal2022open} the hypothesis is stated for all balanced
graphs with fewer than $|J|$ labels; the proof of (5.50) uses it only at
proper divisors of $u_{J,\mathbf d}$, since apart from the coordinates at
the balanced graphs of $(J,\mathbf d)$ these are the only coordinates on
which $A(J,\mathbf d,\cdot)$ depends.  The first term of each formula is
written there as the sum of $\nu_{\Gamma_{J,p},\mathbf d}$ and the difference
$\nu^{\mathfrak s}_{\Gamma_{J,p},\mathbf d}-\nu_{\Gamma_{J,p},\mathbf d}$.
The zero counts $h_{p,\mathbf d}$ can be
prescribed by \cite[Lem.~5.17]{grossetal2022open}.

\begin{theorem}[Relative lifting of canonical families]
\label{thm:relative-endpoint-lifting}
Let $\mathbb D\subset\mathbb D'$ and let
$\mathfrak s\in\mathcal C(\mathbb D)$, where $\mathbb D=(I,\mathscr P,\mathcal E)$
and $\mathbb D'=(I',\mathscr P',\mathcal E')$.  Prescribe a value at every balanced
graph datum in $\mathcal E'\setminus\mathcal E$, assuming that these values
and the invariants of $\mathfrak s$ at the balanced graph data in
$\mathcal E$ are the restriction of a chamber index.  Then there exists
$\mathfrak s'\in\mathcal C(\mathbb D')$ realizing those values and
restricting to $\mathfrak s$.  If the input and target index are
symmetric, the extension may be chosen symmetric.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct the new data with smooth connected graphs in the order of
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-GKT-closure}.  Since $\mathcal E'$ is
assembling-closed, the associativity of assembling prescribes compatible
boundary values, which Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-multisection} extends.  Define the
other new data by their prescribed pullbacks and assemblies.

Process the supports of the new balanced graph data by increasing number of
labels, following the proof of \cite[Thm.~5.13]{grossetal2022open}.  For one
support $u=u_{J,\mathbf d}$, temporarily extend the partial family by the
completion to all graph data above.  Proper-divisor coordinates have already been fixed, so the
chamber equations reduce to the one weighted linear relation displayed above
among the coordinates at the balanced graph data of the present support.  The
critical sections have rank one greater than the dimension and are nowhere
zero.  On the injectively labelled smooth-interior cover,
\cite[Lem.~5.17]{grossetal2022open} supplies compactly supported
modifications with prescribed rational signed zero counts.  The formulas
\cite[(5.52)--(5.54)]{grossetal2022open} recalled above are triangular in
these counts: the coefficient $4k_1(\Lambda_{J,p+1})$ of $h_{p+1,\mathbf d}$
in $o'_{J,p,\mathbf d}$ is nonzero, because $k_1(\Lambda_{J,p+1})=r_J+4p+1$.
Choose $h_{1,\mathbf d},\dots,h_{N,\mathbf d}$ successively so that the
invariants of $\Gamma_{J,0},\dots,\Gamma_{J,N-1}$ take their prescribed
values; the last balanced coordinate is then forced by the weighted chamber
relation \cite[(5.50)]{grossetal2022open}, whose weights are nonzero.
Reapply the relative extension lemma and reassemble the affected data.

A change made for a support $u$ affects only data whose support is a multiple
of $u$.  Suppose such a datum $\delta$ were old.  Its support lies in the old
monomial set by condition~(3) of Definition~\ref{def:finite-partial-system},
so $u$ lies there by divisor closure, and condition~(4) places all balanced
and critical graph data of support $u$ in the old system.  Then $u$ is not the
support of a new balanced graph datum, a contradiction.  Hence the original
family is unchanged.  For symmetry, make choices orbitwise and take normalized
finite orbit-unions; on old orbits these equal the old invariant section.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Relative simple-homotopy lifting]
\label{thm:relative-homotopy-lifting}
Let $\mathbb D\subset\mathbb D'$, let
$\mathfrak s^0,\mathfrak s^1\in\mathcal C(\mathbb D')$, and suppose
\[
 H\in\mathcal H\bigl(
 \mathbb D;\mathfrak s^0|_{\mathbb D},\mathfrak s^1|_{\mathbb D};
 (\mathfrak t(J))_{J\subseteq I}\bigr).
\]
Then the assignment $J\mapsto\mathfrak t(J)$ extends to an injective assignment
on the subsets of $I'$, and $H$ extends to an element of
$\mathcal H\bigl(\mathbb D';\mathfrak s^0,\mathfrak s^1;
(\mathfrak t(J))_{J\subseteq I'}\bigr)$ which agrees with the old homotopies,
equals $\mathfrak s^0$ and $\mathfrak s^1$ on the time collars, and keeps the
collars of the old homotopies.  In particular, all old subset times are
retained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an old component whose rank equals the dimension and with
$k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$, the times at which a slice
$\{\mathfrak t\}\times(\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma\setminus\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma)$
is not transverse form a measure-zero set
by Sard's theorem on the finitely many zero-set strata.  Case~(c) in the
proof of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open} examines the relevant boundary
strata of such a component and shows that on each of them the assembled data
either vanish nowhere or vanish only at the time $\mathfrak t(A)$ of a base
component whose rank exceeds its dimension by one, $A$ being its label set.
Hence the boundary values of such a component vanish only at times
$\mathfrak t(A)$ with $\varnothing\neq A\subseteq I(\Gamma)$.  Choose the new
subset times distinctly, away from the old values and these exceptional
sets, and outside the time collars.  No old homotopy must be changed.

Induct on $\dim\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$, choosing only new data with smooth
connected graphs.  The values at $\mathfrak t=0$ and $\mathfrak t=1$ are
prescribed.  On a boundary stratum $\Lambda$ without a contracted boundary
half-edge use the assembly $F_\Lambda^*\bigl(\boxplus_\Xi H^\Xi\bigr)$ of
the homotopies $H^\Xi$ of the connected components $\Xi$ of $B\Lambda$, as
in \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}; on disconnected faces take external sums.
These prescriptions agree at corners by associativity of assembling.  On a
boundary stratum $\Lambda$ with a contracted boundary half-edge, the proof
of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open} uses the linear homotopy between the
components of $\mathfrak s^0$ and $\mathfrak s^1$; at a corner with a
stratum of the preceding kind it need not agree with the assembled homotopy,
which need not be linear in time.  We use instead a homotopy on $\Lambda$
that agrees with the assembled homotopies near the corners, is positive at
the contracted boundary half-edges in the sense of
\cite[Def.~3.18(1)]{grossetal2022open}, and equals the components of
$\mathfrak s^0$ and $\mathfrak s^1$ on the time collars, for instance the
combination, by a partition of unity, of a positive extension of the corner
values near the corners with
$(1-c(\mathfrak t))s^0_{\Lambda,\mathbf d}+c(\mathfrak t)s^1_{\Lambda,\mathbf d}$;
it is positive because this positivity is preserved by convex
combinations.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-multisection} with all old data and boundary
data prescribed.  For a component $\Gamma$ whose rank exceeds the dimension
by one and with $k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq2$, the prescribed data are nowhere zero
on $[0,1]\times\partial\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$: on irrelevant strata by the
condition on irrelevant components and positivity, and on relevant strata by
the argument of case~(b) in the proof of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open},
a dimension count showing that the base of every relevant boundary stratum
has a component whose rank exceeds its dimension by at least two, or two
components whose ranks exceed their dimensions by one and whose label sets
are distinct.
That argument uses only the conditions above for the base components, all of
smaller dimension, together with \cite[Lem.~3.39]{grossetal2022open} and the
injectivity of $J\mapsto\mathfrak t(J)$, and old base components satisfy these
conditions for their old times, which are retained.  We then make the
homotopy transverse.  It has finitely many zeros, and after a small
perturbation no two of them have the same image under the projection to
$\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$.  A diffeomorphism of $[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$
that commutes with this projection and is the identity near the boundary of
$[0,1]\times\mathcal{PM}_\Gamma$ and near the positive boundary then moves
all zeros to the assigned time $\mathfrak t(I(\Gamma))$, as in the proof of
\cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}.  For a component whose rank equals the dimension and with
$k_{12}(\Gamma)\leq1$, the prescribed data are transverse on the boundary
strata, by the argument of case~(c) there, which derives transversality on
each relevant boundary stratum from the conditions above on the components
of its base.  For base components $\Xi_1$ of
this kind and $\Xi_2$ of the preceding kind with $A=I(\Xi_2)$, that argument
uses the transversality of the interior slice of $H^{\Xi_1}$ at
$\mathfrak t(A)$, which holds for old $\Xi_1$ by the choice of the new times, and
the absence of boundary zeros of $H^{\Xi_1}$ at $\mathfrak t(A)$, which holds
because these zeros lie at times $\mathfrak t(A')$ with
$A'\subseteq I(\Xi_1)$, hence $A'\neq A$.  The prescribed data vanish only at
isolated points of codimension-one strata, at times $\mathfrak t(A)$ with
$\varnothing\neq A\subseteq I(\Gamma)$; near each of them extend the data into
the interior so that the restriction to the interior slice is transverse, as
in the proof of \cite[Lem.~3.40(3)(b)]{grossetal2022open}.  Then make the
slices at the assigned times transverse, prescribe the data on collars around
them, and perturb on the complement.  Irrelevant
components and components whose rank exceeds the dimension by at least two
are made nowhere zero relative to the prescribed data, by positivity and by
transversality respectively.  On the other relevant components the homotopy
is extended arbitrarily relative to the prescribed data, as at the end of the
proof of \cite[Lem.~3.40]{grossetal2022open}.  Automorphism orbit-unions preserve the
assigned time because it depends only on the invariant label set.  The
homotopies on the remaining data are then the forced pullbacks and
assemblies.
\end{proof}

The inductive step of this proof, applied to the missing data with smooth
connected graphs in the order of Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-GKT-closure} after the
canonical families at $\mathfrak t=0$ and $\mathfrak t=1$ have been completed,
gives the corresponding completion of homotopies to all graph data.

\subsection{The compatible union}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization} assembles the
finite constructions above along an increasing sequence of systems.
Restriction is taken along assembling-closed systems because, by the
assembling relation \eqref{eq:GKT-assembling}, a canonical family is
determined on the boundary of each moduli space by its multisections on other
graph data; on such systems, restriction to a smaller system is the
forgetful map.  A system contains all balanced and critical graph data of
each of its supports that carries a balanced graph, because the chamber
conditions couple the coordinates at all balanced graphs of a support through
one weighted relation, these coordinates are adjusted through the critical
graphs of the same support, and this completeness ensures that realizing the
prescribed invariants at the balanced graphs of a new support never alters
an old datum (proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-endpoint-lifting}).  The rays that carry a
nontrivial factor of the GKT transition on some system $\mathbb D_n$ form a
countable set, whose slope order
need not be the order of a sequence; the complementary open intervals of a
Cantor set in $[0,1]$, ordered by position, form a countable dense linear
order without endpoints, into which every countable linear order embeds.
One such embedding, fixed for all systems, gives each of these rays its own
time interval, the intervals being pairwise disjoint and ordered by slope.
Passing from $\mathbb D_n$ to $\mathbb D_{n+1}$ one carries along the
canonical families that realize the extremal and the intermediate chamber
indices, and the homotopies between consecutive families on their intervals;
the data on $\mathbb D_{n+1}$ restrict to those on $\mathbb D_n$, and the
families and the homotopy of Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization}
are the inverse limit of these data.

The proof below uses the companion paper \cite{wuebben2026boundary} only for
three facts in every finite divisor-closed quotient: the two extremal chamber
indices are unique \cite[Thm.~1.1]{wuebben2026boundary}, the enlarged
transition group acts freely \cite[Lem.~2.5]{wuebben2026boundary}, and the
transition between the extremal indices has a unique increasing-slope
factorization \cite[Thm.~1.1 and Prop.~3.1]{wuebben2026boundary}.  The finite
homotopies themselves are constructed by
Theorems~\ref{thm:relative-endpoint-lifting}
and~\ref{thm:relative-homotopy-lifting}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:compatible-GKT-realization}]
Choose increasing finite label sets $I_n$ exhausting $I_\infty$,
finite divisor-closed sets of labelled monomials in the labels of $I_n$
whose union is the set of all labelled monomials, and an enumeration of
all graph data.  Let $\mathscr P_n$ be the smallest divisor-closed set invariant under
the twist-preserving permutations of $I_n$ that contains $\mathscr P_{n-1}$, the $n$th
of these monomial sets, and the supports of the first $n$ graph data with
labels in $I_n$.  Let $\mathcal E_n$ consist of $\mathcal E_{n-1}$, these
graph data, and all balanced and critical graph data of every support in $\mathscr P_n$
that carries a balanced graph, closed under taking assembling constituents
and under the twist-preserving permutations of $I_n$.  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-GKT-closure}, $\mathcal E_n$ is finite and every
support of a datum in $\mathcal E_n$ lies in $\mathscr P_n$.  This gives the systems
$\mathbb D_n=(I_n,\mathscr P_n,\mathcal E_n)$.

The union over all $n$ of the finite sets of nonzero ray factors on the
systems $\mathbb D_n$ is countable.  Embed its slope order into the ordered set of complementary
open intervals of a Cantor set in $[0,1]$; each ray factor thereby receives an
open time interval.  Inductively apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-endpoint-lifting} to all intermediate canonical
families and Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-homotopy-lifting} to the elementary
homotopies, each of which realizes one ray factor on its interval, always
relative to the previous system (for $n=1$ relative to the empty system, so
that there are no old data).

Fix $\delta\in\mathbb D_{n_0}$.  Only the finitely many ray factors on the
system $\mathbb D_{n_0}$ can be nonconstant on it: a factor introduced on a
later system is extended relative to the constant homotopy on the old system, which lies in
$\mathcal H$ for any distinct times: its components of rank exceeding the
dimension are nowhere zero, and those of rank equal to the dimension have
nowhere-zero boundary values and transverse slices.  This
finiteness proves Part~(2).  On the interval of such a factor use its
elementary homotopy; elsewhere use the constant value reached after the
finitely many such intervals to the left.  Constant collars make these
pieces join smoothly.  Every assembling identity involves finitely many
graph data and hence holds on some system $\mathbb D_n$.

The completion to all graph data permits application of the argument of
\cite[Thm.~5.13]{grossetal2022open} on each system $\mathbb D_n$.  Uniqueness of the
factorization, support by support, identifies the jump with the required ray
factor in every finite quotient.  Since the transition maps are forgetful
maps, the compatible restrictions form an inverse limit in sets.
\end{proof}

Enlargement of the labelled system leaves the realization on the old system
unchanged.
